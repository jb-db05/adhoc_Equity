1. What the company does

Enbridge is North America's largest energy infrastructure company. Its Mainline, the world's longest crude oil pipeline system, carries about 3 million barrels a day from western Canada to US refineries, and liquids pipelines provide about half of group EBITDA. It also runs major US gas pipelines such as Texas Eastern, gas utilities in Ontario and, since the purchase of three Dominion utilities, in Ohio, Utah and North Carolina, plus a smaller renewable power business. About 98% of EBITDA comes from regulated or long-term contracted assets.

2. Why it could do well

The secured growth backlog reached a record CAD 41bn in July, much of it in US gas pipelines and utilities serving LNG exports and data centres. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of CAD 20.2 to 20.8bn of adjusted EBITDA and CAD 5.70 to 6.10 of distributable cash flow per share, and targets about 5% annual growth in both after 2026. The Tallgrass and Salt Creek purchases extend its crude network to the Rockies and the Permian, and should add to earnings from 2027.

3. What sets it apart

Scale and contracts. Few companies own hard-to-replicate networks in both crude and gas, and its cash flows have been resilient through oil price cycles. The dividend has risen for 31 consecutive years. At CAD 3.88 a year, it absorbs about two thirds of the midpoint of 2026 distributable cash flow guidance (Syz calculation), a comfortable level for a pipeline company.

4. Is the share cheap?

No. At 23.6x forward earnings, Enbridge trades well above its median of 18.5x since 2021. Consensus forward EPS has slipped from about CAD 3.05 in January 2023 to CAD 2.87 today, while the multiple rose from about 18x to 24x: the share's rise since 2023 came entirely from re-rating. In the second quarter, EBITDA grew 2.8% but adjusted EPS fell to CAD 0.63 from 0.65, as higher interest and depreciation absorbed the growth. The share has fallen 16% since its July high, which removes some of the excess. The 5.7% yield, rather than earnings growth, is what an investor is buying.

5. Main risks

Leverage first: debt stands at 5.1x EBITDA, above the 4.5x to 5.0x target range. That prompted a CAD 3.0bn share issue in September (44.7m shares at CAD 66.85, about 2% dilution) before Tallgrass contributes. Per-share growth could keep lagging total growth if more equity is needed. Line 5 still faces legal challenges in Michigan and Wisconsin. As a high-yield stock, Enbridge is also sensitive to rising bond yields.

6. What to watch next

Third-quarter results on 6 November, with the leverage update after the share issue. The December dividend announcement and 2027 guidance. The closing of the Tallgrass deal. The handover from Greg Ebel to Michele Harradence, head of the gas utilities business, as CEO on 1 January 2027.

CONCLUSION

We see Enbridge as a dependable income holding rather than a growth story. The dividend looks secure, but per-share earnings have not grown in three years and leverage is stretched, so the valuation leaves little room for disappointment. An investor holding it for income could keep it as a core position, noting the overlap if the portfolio also holds Williams, TC Energy or other pipeline names. An investor looking for capital gains could find better growth elsewhere. An investor without exposure who wants the yield could build a position gradually rather than in one go.
