US midstream: record volumes, steady income

Volumes at record levels

US pipeline operators are moving record volumes of gas, NGLs and crude oil. Permian output keeps growing with oil above USD 100 a barrel, Gulf Coast LNG terminals are taking more gas, and data centres are signing direct gas supply deals. For the largest networks, this means more fees at little extra cost.

Most of these companies are paid for capacity rather than for the commodity, so their cash flows are steadier than those of producers, and many return the bulk of that cash to investors.

Partnerships: high yields, different rules

Several large operators, including Energy Transfer, are organised as master limited partnerships (MLPs). They issue units rather than shares and pay distributions rather than dividends. MLPs usually offer higher yields than corporations, but they come with specific US tax reporting (a K-1 form) and withholding rules for non-US holders.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

MLPs suit income-focused investors who can handle the tax administration. Before investing, a non-US investor should check how the custodian handles MLP units, since US withholding can apply to both distributions and sale proceeds.
