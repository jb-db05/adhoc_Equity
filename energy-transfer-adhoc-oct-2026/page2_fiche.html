1. What the company does

Energy Transfer owns one of the largest and most diversified energy networks in the United States, with more than 140,000 miles of pipelines in 44 states. It gathers and processes gas; transports gas, crude oil and NGLs; runs fractionation plants and export terminals such as Nederland in Texas; and holds stakes in Sunoco and USA Compression. Around 90% of earnings come from fees, and no single segment provides more than about a third of EBITDA.

2. Why it could do well

Results are strong. Second-quarter adjusted EBITDA rose 31% to USD 5.07bn on record volumes, and the 2026 guidance was raised to USD 18.8 to 19.1bn. The Hugh Brinson pipeline, carrying Permian gas to north Texas, is in service, and Energy Transfer has agreed to build facilities supplying a 900 MW expansion of Crusoe's AI data centre campus in Abilene. The USD 2.6bn purchase of Vaquero Midstream, due to close in the fourth quarter, adds 675 MMcf/d of Delaware Basin processing and is expected to add to cash flow per unit from the start.

3. What sets it apart

Scale and the payout. Few networks match its reach across gas, crude and NGLs, which lets it sell integrated services and capture extra margin along the chain. Distributable cash flow of USD 2.59bn in the second quarter covered the distribution about twice (Syz calculation), leaving room to fund growth and keep raising the payout within the targeted 3% to 5% a year.

4. Is the share cheap?

Reasonably priced. At 11.9x forward earnings, the units trade above their median of 10.1x since 2021, but far below the 27x to 29x of Targa and Williams. Since January 2023, consensus forward EPS has risen only about 9%, from USD 1.59 to USD 1.73, while the multiple went from about 7x to 12x: the re-rating has largely played out, and further gains would need earnings growth. The 6.6% distribution yield is the main attraction, and it is well covered.

5. Main risks

Capital discipline is the main question. Energy Transfer has a history of large acquisitions and heavy spending, with organic growth capital of USD 5.6 to 5.9bn this year and the large Desert Southwest gas pipeline ahead. The Vaquero deal is paid partly in 33.3m new units. Legal challenges around the Dakota Access pipeline remain an overhang. Part of the 2026 strength reflects high oil prices and wide regional price gaps, which may not last. For non-US investors, MLP tax reporting and withholding add cost and complexity.

6. What to watch next

Third-quarter results on 3 November, including the ramp-up of Hugh Brinson. The closing of the Vaquero acquisition. The second phase of Hugh Brinson in early 2027. The 2027 guidance and capital budget early next year.

CONCLUSION

We see Energy Transfer as the most attractive income vehicle among the US pipeline groups we have reviewed: the payout is high, well covered and growing, and the valuation is undemanding. The trade-offs are an appetite for large projects and deals, and the tax complexity of the MLP structure. An investor seeking income who can hold MLP units could build or keep a position, sized with any overlap with Targa, Williams or Enbridge in mind. An investor for whom K-1 reporting or US withholding is a problem could prefer corporate peers, accepting a lower yield.
