1. What the company does

Williams moves about one third of US natural gas. Its core is Transco, the country's largest gas pipeline system by volume, running from the Gulf Coast to New York. Most earnings come from regulated, fee-based transport and storage contracts, so they depend little on the gas price. Williams also gathers and processes gas in the Marcellus, Haynesville and Rockies basins, runs a gas marketing arm and, since 2025, builds gas power plants for data centres ("Power Innovation").

2. Why it could do well

Growth is visible and contracted. In August, management raised 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to USD 8.3 to 8.5bn and lifted its long-term target to 11%+ annual EBITDA growth through 2030. The first phase of Socrates, a 400 MW project for Meta in Ohio, is in service, with phase two due in the fourth quarter. Williams has announced more than 2.6 GW of power projects and cites a pipeline of over 6 GW. The USD 5.5bn purchase of Momentum Midstream, closed on 3 September, adds Haynesville gathering capacity of 6 Bcf/d feeding Gulf Coast LNG and industrial demand.

3. What sets it apart

Transco cannot be replicated: new interstate pipelines take years to permit, so existing routes gain value as demand grows. Williams also funds growth smartly. In July, a Blackstone-led group committed USD 5.34bn for 49% of five power projects, covering its share of their capital spending and paying Williams about USD 0.9bn upfront, while Williams keeps control and a buyback right. This protects the balance sheet, at the cost of sharing future profits.

4. Is the share cheap?

No. At 29.4x forward earnings, Williams trades well above its median of 22.7x since 2021. Since January 2023, consensus forward EPS has risen by about a third, from USD 1.80 to USD 2.44, while the multiple went from about 18x to 29x: most of the share's doubling came from re-rating, not earnings. Even if earnings per share grew at the 11% EBITDA target, it would take about two and a half years at today's price to bring the multiple back to its median (Syz calculation). The share has stalled since February, a sign that the power story is largely priced. The 2.9% dividend yield, covered more than twice, offers some support.

5. Main risks

Valuation is the first: at this multiple, a delay in new power contracts could weigh on the share even if results are solid. The guidance increase came from the Momentum deal rather than the base business. The 2026 growth capital budget of USD 7.3 to 7.9bn brings execution and permitting risk on large projects such as the Northeast Supply Enhancement pipeline. Leverage rises to about 3.9x at year-end, and about USD 2bn of new shares issued to Momentum's sellers could weigh on the stock as they are sold. The marketing arm adds quarterly noise.

6. What to watch next

Third-quarter results on 2 November, the first with Momentum. Any new behind-the-meter contract with a data centre customer. Completion of Socrates phase two in the fourth quarter. The 2027 guidance, expected early in 2027, for the pace of growth and leverage.

CONCLUSION

We like Williams as a business: a unique network, contracted growth and a well-covered, rising dividend. We are more cautious on the share, which already prices much of the power opportunity at 29x earnings. An investor who holds it could keep a core position for its quality and income, without adding at this level, especially if the portfolio already carries other data centre or US power names. An investor without exposure could wait for the third-quarter results and evidence of new power contracts, or build a position gradually rather than in one go.
