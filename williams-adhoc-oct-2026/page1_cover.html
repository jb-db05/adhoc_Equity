US gas infrastructure: weak gas prices, strong demand for pipes

A cheap commodity, a valuable network

US natural gas remains inexpensive. The Henry Hub benchmark traded around USD 3.1 to 3.2 per MMBtu in mid-October, with storage above its five-year average. For producers this is a headwind; for pipeline owners it matters much less, because most of their revenue comes from fees for reserved capacity rather than from the gas price.

What drives pipeline value is demand for new capacity. Two sources stand out: liquefied natural gas exports from the Gulf Coast, and gas-fired power for data centres, whose electricity needs are growing faster than grids can connect them.

Pipeline companies move into power

Some operators now build gas power plants directly at data centre sites, "behind the meter", so customers do not have to wait for a grid connection. Owning the gas supply and the pipeline gives them a cost and timing edge. Williams is among the most advanced of the large US operators in this field, with its first project for Meta in Ohio already partly in service.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

The sector offers fee-based cash flows with a growth kicker from power demand. The question for an investor is price: the market already pays a premium for the data centre theme, so the next leg of returns depends on new contracts being signed, not on the theme itself.
