1. What the company does

Disney operates three segments: Entertainment (Disney+, Hulu, ABC, linear networks, studios), Sports (ESPN) and Experiences (parks, resorts, cruise, consumer products). Earnings are driven by streaming subscriptions and advertising, park attendance and per-capita spending, and affiliate fees. Cash content spending rises 9% to USD 26bn in FY27, with sports at about 40%. A USD 60bn, ten-year Experiences capex plan is under way.

  • Experiences keeps compounding. Record global attendance in FY26; new capacity supports pricing.

3. What sets it apart

  • Franchise IP (Disney, Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars) earns money across film, streaming, parks and merchandise.
  • ESPN live rights (NFL, NBA, College Football Playoff, Super Bowl in February 2027) keep distributors and advertisers dependent on it.
  • Pricing power: tiered price increases have pushed customers toward bundles, and park capacity takes years to replicate.

4. Is the share cheap?

At 16.0x FY27E the stock trades at a discount to the US market, reflecting the conglomerate structure more than asset quality. Two caveats apply.

  • Part of the EPS growth comes from buybacks. Guided segment operating income grows 8%; the buyback (about 4% of market value) supplies most of the gap to double-digit EPS growth.
  • Payouts exceed cash generation. Buybacks and dividends total about USD 12.5bn against USD 11bn of guided FCF, so the balance sheet funds the difference while capex stays high.

5. Main risks

  • Linear TV decline erodes ESPN affiliate fees and ABC advertising faster than streaming can replace them.
  • The consumer: parks are cyclical, and Shanghai and Hong Kong are already soft.
  • Content and costs: a lighter FY27 film slate, franchise fatigue and rising sports-rights costs.
  • Generative AI cuts both ways: it could lower production costs, but makes it harder to control the IP.

6. What to watch next

  • F1Q27 results (early February 2027): SVOD margin holding at or above 13%, and domestic per-capita spending.
  • Direction of FY27 EPS revisions, which start at the guidance floor.
  • Investor Day (May 2027): explicit FCF, ROIC and streaming-margin targets, and a framework for returns on Experiences capex.
Conclusion

Disney is a high-quality franchise at a reasonable multiple, but the easiest part of the recovery is now priced in. Consensus sits at the bottom of guidance and the skew is balanced to slightly positive. A broader re-rating would most likely need Investor Day to turn the "One Disney" strategy into hard cash-flow targets. A neutral to modest overweight looks appropriate. With the stock near its 2024–25 highs and well above its 50-day average, building exposure gradually could be preferable to adding all at once.

What would strengthen our view
  • Streaming margins tracking toward the mid-teens ahead of 2028
  • Investor Day sets FCF and ROIC commitments
What would change our view
  • Experiences operating income growth falls below mid-single digits
  • FY27 EPS revisions turn negative
