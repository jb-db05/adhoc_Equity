The shares are back above their 40-day average (EUR 392.6) but far below a still-falling 200-day average (EUR 455.6). A close above EUR 416, the early-November high, could signal a stabilisation; EUR 380, near the October low, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

LVMH is the world's largest luxury group, with about 75 houses in five divisions: Fashion & Leather Goods (Louis Vuitton, Dior, Loro Piana, Celine), Perfumes & Cosmetics, Watches & Jewelry (Tiffany, Bulgari), Selective Retailing (Sephora) and Wines & Spirits (Moët & Chandon, Hennessy). Revenue was EUR 80.8bn in 2025.

2. Why it could do well

The portfolio spreads risk: Sephora, jewellery and spirits are growing while leather goods struggle. The group also has levers of its own: creative renewal at Dior, newness in the ranges, cost discipline and a leaner portfolio, with the sale of Marc Jacobs agreed in May and the stake in SirDavis whisky sold in July. Operating free cash flow reached EUR 11.3bn in 2025.

3. What sets it apart

Few groups own as many brands with pricing power, control their own stores and production, and can keep investing through a downturn. Net debt of EUR 8.2bn at the end of June was less than half of 2025 operating profit. Control by the Arnault family gives the group a long-term horizon.

4. Cheap, or a value trap?

Cheap on history, at 16.8 times expected earnings against a ten-year median of 22.9 times and 19.5 times over twenty years. The risk lies in the earnings. Consensus still expects a recovery in 2027, while Fashion & Leather Goods fell 2% over nine months and industry studies show the luxury customer base shrinking since 2022, as price increases pushed aspirational buyers away. The shares look cheap if the core division stabilises in 2027, and would be a value trap if its decline continues.

5. Main risks

A longer slump in China, where consumer confidence is weak and the Molly Tea dispute has hurt brand image. Aspirational customers moving to cheaper brands or second-hand. A strong euro, which turned 2% organic growth into a 2% reported decline over nine months. Tariffs and geopolitics weighing on travel and spending. Succession: Bernard Arnault is 77, and the age limit for his role was raised to 85 in 2025.

6. What to watch next

Full-year results in late January 2027, especially the Fashion & Leather Goods margin; the trend among Chinese customers at home and abroad; the reception of the new Dior collections and newness at Louis Vuitton; completion of the Marc Jacobs sale and any further disposals.

CONCLUSION

LVMH's share price is back below its pre-pandemic level and its multiple is close to a ten-year low, while the group remains highly profitable and cash generative. The main question is now earnings: consensus still assumes a 2027 recovery that the core division has yet to show. Signs of stabilisation in Fashion & Leather Goods and in China would be needed for the low multiple to turn into a recovery in the share price.
