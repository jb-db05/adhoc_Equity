A low multiple, with earnings still at risk

Growth has stalled in the core division

LVMH's revenue reached EUR 18.50bn in the third quarter of 2026, up 1% on an organic basis after 3% in the second quarter. Fashion & Leather Goods, home to Louis Vuitton and Dior, fell 5% as Chinese demand weakened. Asia excluding Japan declined, while the United States, Europe and Japan grew. Over nine months revenue was EUR 57.1bn, up 2% organically but down 2% as reported because of the strong euro.

The other divisions held up. Sephora led Selective Retailing to 8% organic growth in the quarter, Wines & Spirits grew 6%, and Watches & Jewelry grew 8% over nine months on the strength of jewellery. In China, a trademark case won by Louis Vuitton against the tea chain Molly Tea set off a social-media backlash in July; management said the effect was hard to size but likely negative early in the quarter.

Profitability is resilient for now. The first-half operating margin was 22.5%, and Fashion & Leather Goods still earned 34.2% on its sales. That division produced about 70% of group operating profit, which is why its trend drives the share price.

The share price has priced in a lot

At EUR 402.20 the shares are down 38% since the start of the year, while the Stoxx Europe 600 is up 8%, and 55% below their April 2023 record of EUR 902. They now trade below their level at the end of 2019, before the pandemic.

Almost all of this year's fall comes from the valuation. Consensus earnings for the next twelve months are unchanged since January, while the multiple dropped from 26.8 to 16.8 times, close to its ten-year low of 15.5 times reached in October.

That stable estimate deserves caution. It now mostly covers 2027 and implies earnings per share about 10% above the EUR 21.8 of 2025, while organic growth runs at 1% to 2% and the core division is shrinking. If the recovery comes later, estimates could fall and the multiple would look less low.

What this means for investors

The valuation already discounts a long downturn, and the group keeps generating cash, with a 3.2% dividend yield. What is missing is evidence that Fashion & Leather Goods has stopped declining. The full-year results in late January 2027 and the reception of the new Dior collections are the next data points.
