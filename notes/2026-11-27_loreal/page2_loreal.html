The shares trade well above their 40-day (EUR 421.8) and 200-day averages after the jump that followed the third-quarter release. A close above EUR 457, the record close of May 2024, could open a new range; EUR 422, the late-October low, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

L'Oréal is the world's largest beauty company. It has four divisions: Consumer Products (L'Oréal Paris, Garnier, Maybelline), L'Oréal Luxe (Lancôme, Yves Saint Laurent, Armani and, since April, Creed and the Kering brands), Professional Products (Kérastase, L'Oréal Professionnel) and Dermatological Beauty (La Roche-Posay, CeraVe, Vichy). Nine-month 2026 sales: EUR 35.3bn.

2. Why it could do well

Beauty is a steady-growth sector and L'Oréal is its world leader. A spread across categories, price points and regions has let it outgrow the market for years, and in the third quarter every division grew. Fragrance, premium haircare and dermatological skincare are growing fastest, and emerging markets such as India and Indonesia offer a long runway. The 50-year Gucci licence is expected to start in mid-2027, once Coty's licence ends early on 30 June 2027.

3. What sets it apart

Scale in research, marketing and distribution, and an early lead in e-commerce and digital tools. Margins are at a record 21.3% even with higher marketing spending. The balance sheet absorbed about EUR 8bn of deals in 2026 (Kering Beauté and a Galderma stake raised to 20%). Leverage was 1.2 times at end-June; management expects below one times by year end.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not any more. At 29.3 times expected earnings the shares trade in line with their ten-year median of 29.5 times. Since mid-2024 the range has been 24 to 30 times, and the shares now sit at its top. The trailing dividend yield is 1.6%, with EUR 7.20 paid for 2025, 57% of earnings. From here, the path depends on rising earnings.

5. Main risks

China regularly eats into the group's growth: it has recovered but stays competitive, with online discounting and strong local and Korean brands. North America is also weaker, at 2.2% like-for-like in the quarter, and Consumer Products is growing slowly. US litigation: over 12,000 lawsuits on hair relaxers at the end of June, a suit by the State of Arizona in September, and about 760 talc cases, which press reports say L'Oréal is looking to transfer. A higher multiple makes the shares more sensitive to any slowdown.

6. What to watch next

Full-year results in February 2027; whether China holds up and whether Consumer Products and North America pick up; the integration of Kering Beauté and the start of the Gucci licence; the course of US litigation and any transaction on talc liabilities.

CONCLUSION

We remain constructive on beauty given its steady growth, with L'Oréal once again outperforming the market as all divisions expand and first-half margins reach a record. The shares have followed suit, rising 21% this year to near record highs and the upper end of their two-year valuation range, leaving limited room for further multiple expansion. From here, upside is likely to be driven primarily by earnings growth. China, North America and US litigation remain the key factors to watch.
