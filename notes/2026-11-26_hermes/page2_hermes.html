The share price is below both its 40-day (EUR 1,303) and 200-day (EUR 1,578) averages, after a 45% fall from the January high to the low of 20 November. A close above EUR 1,357, the 21 October high, could be a first sign of stabilisation; EUR 1,225.5, the low of the year, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

Hermès designs and makes luxury goods in its own workshops, mostly in France: Leather Goods and Saddlery (Birkin, Kelly), Ready-to-wear and Accessories, Silk and Textiles, Perfumes, Watches, Jewellery and Home. It sells through its own stores, which keeps control of prices and the customer relationship.

2. Why it could do well

Leather Goods grew 10.8% in the third quarter in a weak luxury market, and the Americas grew 8.8%. Management plans new leather workshops to meet demand, and January 2027 brings the first men's collection by Grace Wales Bonner and the first Couture collection by Nadège Vanhée, which could add momentum. Comparison bases also ease in 2027.

3. What sets it apart

A vertically integrated model: the company makes its own products, limits supply and sells to a loyal, wealthy clientele. Its profitability is the highest in the sector, and it has historically held up better than peers in downturns. Few luxury brands combine this pricing power with such a long waiting list for their best products.

4. Is the share cheap?

Cheaper than it has been in ten years, but not cheap in absolute terms. The shares trade on 26.8 times expected earnings, against a ten-year average of 44.6 times and a five-year average of 44.7 times. Expected earnings per share are about 1% below their level at the end of 2025, so the fall in the share price comes almost entirely from a lower multiple. The premium over the sector has narrowed but remains significant.

5. Main risks

Growth is slowing in several categories: Watches fell about 6% in the third quarter and Asia excluding Japan was flat. At about EUR 17bn of sales, the group may be too large to avoid the luxury cycle. A weaker yen and other currencies reduce reported revenue. Price increases, about 6% in 2026, could meet resistance if consumer sentiment weakens. Social unrest in France and the Middle East conflict can affect tourism and store traffic.

6. What to watch next

Full-year 2026 results; the January 2027 creative debuts; the pricing decision for 2027; the pace of Leather Goods growth and any recovery in Asia; the evolution of earnings estimates.

CONCLUSION

Hermès remains the most resilient name in luxury, with Leather Goods growing at about 11%. The shares have fallen 40% this year and now trade at their lowest multiple in ten years, while earnings estimates have barely moved. The market is questioning how long a scarcity premium can last as the company grows. Growth outside leather, pricing and the 2026 results will show whether the de-rating is overdone.
