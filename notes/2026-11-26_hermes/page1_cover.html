A growth slowdown, and a share price that has already reacted

Resilient core, softer edges

Hermès generated revenue of EUR 12.3bn in the first nine months of 2026, 5.5% more than a year earlier at constant exchange rates. Growth slowed in the third quarter to 4.3%, from 6.1% in the first half. Leather Goods, the core business, still grew 10.8%, while Watches fell about 6% and Asia excluding Japan was flat. The Americas grew 8.8%.

The slowdown has been punished. The shares peaked at EUR 2,240 on 14 January and trade at EUR 1,277, 43% below that high and 40% lower since the start of the year. The 20 November close of EUR 1,225.50 was the lowest of the year.

Currency also weighs: the group reports growth of 2.9% at current exchange rates for the nine months, well below the constant-rate figure.

Where the debate is now

The question is whether a business built on scarcity can stay out of the luxury cycle as it grows. Supporters point to the pace of Leather Goods and to the quality of the brand; sceptics point to slowing categories outside leather and to the weight of demand-driven rather than supply-constrained products. Pricing, with increases of about 6% in 2026, adds to the sensitivity of consumer sentiment.

What this means for investors

The shares trade on about 26.8 times expected earnings for the next twelve months, against a ten-year average of 44.6 times. That is the lowest level in ten years, and expected earnings per share are 1% lower than at the end of 2025. The multiple has fallen far faster than estimates, so much of the debate is now about how long the premium for quality should last. The next data points are the full-year 2026 results, the creative debuts of January 2027 and any pricing decision for 2027.
