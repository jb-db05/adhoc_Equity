1. What the company does

Kraft Heinz is one of the largest US packaged food groups. Its "WIN BIG" brands, such as Heinz and Philadelphia, make up about 60% of sales; most of the rest is US retail. Emerging markets are a growing part of the business.

2. Why it could do well

The extra spending is starting to show: 31% of revenue is holding or gaining market share this year, against 21% in 2025. The WIN BIG brands are back to consumption growth, and Heinz organic sales in emerging markets rose about 16% in the third quarter. Management raised the low end of its 2026 organic sales guidance and lifted adjusted earnings per share guidance to USD 2.07 to 2.13.

3. What sets it apart

Few food groups own a global brand with the reach of Heinz, which travels well into emerging markets. The group is also reshaping its portfolio: the coffee sale removes a slower business and reduces debt, and the USD 700m investment level is planned to continue in 2027.

4. Cheap, or a value trap?

The market value is USD 28.7bn, against FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of USD 5.85bn, a 23.5% margin. Net debt stood at 3.3 times EBITDA at the end of the third quarter of 2026, a level that leaves limited room for error. The valuation is low for brands of this quality, but it reflects falling volumes: the share is cheap only if the investment brings volumes back.

5. Main risks

North American volumes have been falling for several years; organic sales fell 1.1% in the third quarter, and 3.2% in North America. Cost inflation is expected slightly above 5% in 2027, which would require price increases that could hurt volumes again. The USD 700m programme weighs on margins with no guaranteed return.

6. What to watch next

Fourth-quarter results and 2027 guidance: the volume trend in North America and the margin cost of the investment. Then the closing of the coffee sale, the use of proceeds and the price increases planned to offset inflation.

Conclusion

Kraft Heinz is a recovery case rather than a defensive income stock. The valuation is low and the first signs are encouraging, but the volume turnaround is not yet proven. An investor could wait for stabilising North American volumes before building a position, accepting that part of a re-rating could be missed.
