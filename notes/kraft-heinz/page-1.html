Kraft Heinz is investing an extra USD 700m in 2026 to revive its US retail business and accelerate abroad. Market share trends are improving, but organic sales are still falling and costs are rising.

US packaged food: volumes under pressure

After the price increases, the volume test

After several years of price increases, large US food companies face weaker volumes as consumers trade down or buy less. Many are now reinvesting in marketing, product quality and selective price cuts to win shoppers back, at the cost of lower margins in the short term.

Pruning the portfolio

Food groups are also selling slower brands to focus on their strongest ones. On 3 November 2026, Kraft Heinz agreed to sell its North American coffee business, including Maxwell House, to One Rock Capital Partners for an enterprise value of USD 650m, keeping a 30% stake. Net proceeds of about USD 500m will reduce debt.

What this means for investors

The sector offers defensive revenue but little growth, and investors now reward proof of volume recovery rather than price increases. For Kraft Heinz, the reinvestment plan is the test: if volumes stabilise, the low valuation could re-rate; if not, the share could remain a value trap.
