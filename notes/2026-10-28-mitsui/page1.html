Japanese trading houses enter a new phase

From traders to investment groups

Japan's five large trading houses (Mitsubishi Corporation, Mitsui & Co., Itochu, Sumitomo Corporation and Marubeni) have spent the past decade turning from commodity traders into diversified investment groups. Commodities still carry a large weight in profits. A growing share now comes from businesses such as food, power, infrastructure, finance and consumer goods, which earn steadier returns through the cycle.

Capital discipline as the common thread

Each of the large houses now runs a plan that ties dividends and share buybacks to a return on equity goal. The Tokyo Stock Exchange's push for capital efficiency and the arrival of Berkshire Hathaway as a shareholder in all five houses have reinforced this. At the end of 2025, Berkshire held 10.4% of Mitsui, according to press reports.

A mixed commodity backdrop

Crude oil and iron ore prices could soften in 2027 and 2028 as new supply arrives. Copper stands apart: mine supply remains tight while demand from power grids, electrification and data centres keeps rising. The commodity mix of each house therefore matters more than usual.

Where Mitsui stands

Mitsui is one of the most resource-weighted houses. In the year to March 2026, its Mineral & Metal Resources and Energy segments earned about half of group profit (Syz calculation from company figures). Iron ore, mainly from Australia, is its largest single driver. LNG comes next, with copper a smaller part of the mix.

Profit has eased from its record in the year to March 2023. The first quarter of the current year brought a new quarterly record and covered 32% of the full-year plan, a pace similar to Marubeni's. Mitsui's plan to March 2029 targets profit of JPY 1.1 trillion and a 12% return on equity. It also commits to return around half of cumulative core operating cash flow to shareholders, with a dividend floor.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

Mitsui offers a visible return policy and a strong anchor shareholder. Its earnings, however, lean on iron ore and energy, the commodities where prices could soften in 2027 and 2028, more than on copper. This sets it apart from Marubeni, covered in a separate note of the same date. The half-year results of both houses on 4 November are the next checkpoint.
