1. What the company does

Mitsui is a trading and investment group active in metals and mining, energy, machinery and infrastructure, chemicals, steel products, lifestyle (food, retail, healthcare) and innovation. In the year to March 2026 (FY3/26), profit attributable to shareholders was JPY 834.0 billion and core operating cash flow JPY 978.9 billion. Mineral & Metal Resources earned JPY 253.6 billion (iron ore, coal, copper and JPY 43.5 billion of Vale dividends) and Energy JPY 164.2 billion.

2. Why it could do well

The first quarter of FY3/27 brought record profit of JPY 294.1 billion, up from JPY 191.6 billion a year earlier and 32% of the JPY 920 billion full-year plan. The plan was left unchanged. The medium-term plan targets profit of JPY 1.1 trillion and an ROE of 12% by FY3/29, with a 2030 ambition of more than JPY 1.4 trillion. New capacity should support this. Mozambique LNG, where Mitsui holds 20%, fully restarted in January 2026, with first LNG expected in 2029.

3. What sets it apart

Mitsui has a clear framework for returning cash:

  • around 50% of three-year cumulative core operating cash flow returned to shareholders
  • a dividend floor of JPY 140 a year from FY3/27 to FY3/29, up from JPY 115 in FY3/26
  • a minimum total payout ratio raised to 40% from 37%
  • a buyback of up to JPY 200 billion or 60 million shares from 5 August 2026 to 29 January 2027, of which JPY 11.25 billion had been bought by the end of August

Berkshire Hathaway is a long-term anchor shareholder.

4. Is the share cheap?

Less so than a few years ago. The share has gained about 31% over twelve months. At JPY 4,942, it trades at about 15 times the FY3/27 plan and about 17 times FY3/26 earnings per share of JPY 291.12. It also trades at about 1.5 times book value. The JPY 140 dividend gives a yield of 2.8%. The buyback adds about 1.4% of market value, so total cash returns come to about 4.2% (Syz calculations). The valuation now rests more on Mitsui delivering its 2029 plan than on a discount to assets.

5. Main risks

  • Commodities. Each USD 1 per tonne on iron ore moves annual profit by about JPY 3 billion. Oil and LNG feed through with a lag of up to six months. The plan assumes Brent at USD 78 and a normalisation of the Middle East situation by the second quarter.
  • Yen. Each JPY 1 move against the dollar changes profit by about JPY 4.6 billion. A weaker yen helped the first quarter, and a stronger yen would work the other way.
  • One-off gains. Part of the first-quarter strength came from gains on investments. Innovation & Corporate Development reached 93% of its full-year plan in one quarter, and such gains do not recur every quarter.
  • Projects and geopolitics. Mozambique LNG carries security and execution risk. Mitsui's Russian LNG interests (Sakhalin-2, Arctic LNG 2) remain exposed to sanctions.

6. What to watch next

The first-half results on 4 November 2026 will show whether the JPY 920 billion plan is revised. Other points to follow are the pace of the buyback until January 2027, iron ore and LNG prices, the yen, and any change in Berkshire's stake.

CONCLUSION

Mitsui gives an investor a broad portfolio with commodities at its core, a well-defined return policy with a dividend floor, and an anchor shareholder. Against this, the shares have already re-rated, the first quarter was flattered by one-off gains, and the earnings path still depends heavily on iron ore, energy and the yen. The main question is less the next quarter than how much confidence one places in the 2029 plan, and how much commodity-cycle volatility one is ready to accept.
