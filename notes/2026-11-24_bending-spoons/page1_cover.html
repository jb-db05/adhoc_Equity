A buyer of tired software, now at scale

From app studio to software consolidator

Bending Spoons, based in Milan, buys established digital products that have stopped growing, moves them onto its own technology platform and runs them with far smaller teams. The portfolio includes Evernote, Remini, WeTransfer, Vimeo, AOL, Eventbrite and Tractive. The group listed on Nasdaq on 1 July 2026 at USD 29 per share, raising USD 1.68bn.

Since the listing the pace has stepped up. Bending Spoons bought Airtable for an enterprise value of USD 1.285bn and Miro for USD 1.355bn, both now closed, and on 19 November agreed to buy Mailchimp from Intuit for USD 3.68bn, its largest deal so far.

The prices matter. Airtable was valued at more than USD 11bn in 2021, and Intuit paid about USD 12bn for Mailchimp the same year. Owners of slowing software businesses are accepting a fraction of peak valuations, and very few buyers can both write cheques of this size and run the assets afterwards. That scarcity is the core of the investment case.

Two debates around the shares

The first is durability. Revenue grew 125% in the third quarter, but only 3% organically: acquisitions did the rest. Some older brands are shrinking, and AI assistants could erode products such as note-taking, photo editing or web portals faster than cost savings can compensate.

The second is the gap between adjusted and reported profit. Adjusted operating income was USD 424m in the quarter, reported operating income USD 172m, mostly because of the amortisation of acquired brands and technology. Interest expense of about USD 107m, on the other hand, is paid in cash.

What this means for investors

The shares are a bet that management keeps buying well and keeps lifting margins. That has worked so far and the third quarter was strong. At about 21 times this year's guided adjusted operating income, we think the price already reflects a good part of that success, while Mailchimp still has to be integrated. We would build exposure gradually rather than chase the shares after sharp rallies.
