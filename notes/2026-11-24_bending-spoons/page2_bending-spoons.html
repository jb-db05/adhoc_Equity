1. What the company does

Bending Spoons acquires mature consumer and business software, rebuilds it on a shared platform and runs it with small teams. Revenue is mostly subscriptions, plus advertising at AOL. Guided 2026 revenue is USD 3.07bn to 3.09bn, against USD 1.31bn in 2025. Mailchimp, with USD 313m of revenue in its latest quarter, would add roughly USD 1.25bn a year once closed.

2. Why it could do well

Margins keep rising: the adjusted operating margin reached 54% in the third quarter. Sellers are queuing, as the Airtable, Miro and Mailchimp prices show. And AI tools could make each integration cheaper: management says 98% of code changes are now written fully or partly by AI and that changes per engineer have more than quadrupled since January.

3. What sets it apart

Few acquirers combine the funding to buy at this size with one technology platform to move every product onto. Long-standing shareholders add credibility: Baillie Gifford has invested since 2023, and Berkshire Hathaway bought USD 600m of shares at the IPO, then USD 330m at USD 33 per share, for about 4% in total. Their entry prices are not a floor for the shares.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not obviously. On our numbers the enterprise value is about USD 34bn, some 21 times guided 2026 adjusted operating income, and the shares trade on about 21 times consensus forward earnings. On reported figures the gap is wide: third-quarter diluted EPS was USD 0.13 against USD 0.42 adjusted. Amortisation is a non-cash charge, but it reflects growth the group has to keep buying.

5. Main risks

Organic growth is low: 3% in the third quarter, with Remini taking 2 points off and AOL 1 point. AI could displace older consumer brands. Interest expense rose 163% to about USD 107m; reported operating income covers it 1.6 times. Deep staff cuts after deals (75% at WeTransfer in 2024) carry more risk with business clients such as Airtable, Miro and Mailchimp. The shares are very volatile, above 100% annualised since listing.

6. What to watch next

Fourth-quarter results, with guided revenue of USD 984m to 1.00bn and about 5% organic growth; first margin evidence at Airtable and Miro; the Mailchimp closing, expected in early 2027, and the USD 3.31bn of cash it requires.

CONCLUSION

Bending Spoons is one of the best operators in software turnarounds, and the third quarter showed the model works at a larger scale. The issue is the price. With low single-digit organic growth, the valuation rests on further deals at high returns, partly funded with debt. We would keep any position modest and add on weakness rather than chase rallies. Firm margins at Airtable and Miro and a pick-up in organic growth would strengthen the case.
