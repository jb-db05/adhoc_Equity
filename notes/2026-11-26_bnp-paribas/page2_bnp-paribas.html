The share price is just above its 40-day average (EUR 90.2) and below its 200-day average (EUR 95.5), after a 27% fall from August to October and a partial recovery. A close above EUR 95.8, the 11 November high, could confirm the improvement; EUR 82.6, the low of 22 October, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

BNP Paribas is France's largest bank and one of the largest in Europe. It combines Corporate and Institutional Banking (investment banking, markets and financing), Commercial, Personal Banking and Services (retail banks in France, Belgium, Italy and Luxembourg, plus Arval and leasing) and Investment and Protection Services (asset management, insurance and wealth).

2. Why it could do well

Earnings are growing across divisions, with third-quarter net income up 13.5%. Investment banking is gaining ground, particularly in equities. The bank plans to double pre-tax income in asset management by 2030, helped by the integration of AXA Investment Managers, and to lift returns at its Italian arm BNL. Excess capital above a CET1 ratio of 13% is meant for shareholders, which supports buybacks.

3. What sets it apart

Few European banks combine a top-ranked investment bank with large retail, leasing and asset management businesses in several countries. This mix reduces the weight of any single market, and the bank's capital position is among the strongest of its peers.

4. Is the share cheap?

Against its own history, modestly so. The shares trade on about 7.1 times consensus earnings for the next twelve months, against a ten-year average of 7.7 times and a five-year average of 7.0 times. The comparison is not far from average, but earnings estimates have risen 15% since the end of last year and the multiple has not followed. Bank multiples are structurally low, so the relevant comparison is with the bank's own past and its peers.

5. Main risks

French political and fiscal uncertainty and weaker euro-area growth could affect lending and asset quality. A US jury found the bank liable in October 2025 in a case linked to the Sudanese regime and awarded about USD 21m to three plaintiffs; the bank is appealing, and plaintiffs' lawyers say the ruling could open the way to many more claims. Investment banking revenues can fall when markets turn. BNL's plan relies on a profound change of its Italian business.

6. What to watch next

The full Group 2030 plan, expected early in 2027; the progress of the EUR 1.3bn buyback and further distributions; the completion of the sale of the Moroccan subsidiary BMCI; the Second Circuit appeal in the Sudan case; the evolution of French public finances.

CONCLUSION

BNP Paribas is delivering growing profits, a strong capital position and rising shareholder returns, yet its shares sit 19% below their August high and below their average multiple. The gap reflects caution on French politics and legal risk rather than weaker results. The strategic plan expected in early 2027 and the pace of buybacks are the next tests of whether that caution eases.
