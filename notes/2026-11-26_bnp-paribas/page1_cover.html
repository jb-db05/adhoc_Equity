Earnings are rising faster than the share price

A strong quarter, led by investment banking

BNP Paribas reported third-quarter net income of EUR 3.46bn, 13.5% above a year earlier and ahead of what analysts expected, on revenues of EUR 13.76bn. Equities trading revenue rose about 50%, and pre-tax profit in investment banking rose about 26%. The bank also announced a EUR 1.3bn share buyback, approved by the European Central Bank, which started in November.

The balance sheet is solid. The CET1 ratio, the main measure of capital strength, stood at 12.9% at the end of September, even after the acquisition of the leasing company Athlon, and the bank raised its year-end 2026 target to above 13%. Capital above that level is intended for shareholders, with buybacks playing a large part.

The share price has not followed the earnings. After reaching EUR 112.80 on 10 August, the shares fell about 27% to EUR 82.57 on 22 October, then recovered part of the loss. At EUR 91.00 they are 19% below the August high.

Where the debate is now

Investors are weighing a broad, diversified bank against a difficult home market. French political and fiscal uncertainty weighs on the whole sector, and BNP Paribas is one of the largest holders of French government debt. Its earnings are spread across investment banking, asset management, and retail and commercial banking in several countries, which limits the dependence on France.

Expectations for earnings keep rising: consensus forward earnings per share are EUR 12.88, 15% above the level at the end of 2025.

What this means for investors

The shares trade on about 7.1 times expected earnings, below their ten-year average of 7.7 times, while earnings estimates and capital returns are rising. The next data points are the full Group 2030 plan, expected early in 2027, and the progress of the buyback. French politics and the Sudan court case remain the main sources of uncertainty.
