Viking, Immunovant and Incyte: three very different ways to own biotech risk

16 November 2026 · Syz Research
Market capitalisations are as of 16 November 2026. Company figures are as of 30 September 2026 unless stated.
Important notice

Two of the three companies in this note have no product on the market. Their value rests on clinical trials and regulatory decisions, and their shares can gain or lose 30% or more in a single session on a trial result.

A sector that pays for late-stage proof

Biotech investors are rewarding companies that turn promising early data into late-stage, registrational evidence, and are quick to punish any doubt on safety, dose or commercial differentiation. The three names in this note sit at different points on that path.

Incyte is a profitable commercial company. Its question is not whether its drugs work but whether its pipeline can replace Jakafi, its largest product, which loses US exclusivity in 2028.

Immunovant has no revenue but a de-risked asset: IMVT-1402 has now produced a positive randomised result in difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis, with registrational readouts in Graves' disease and myasthenia gravis due in 2027.

Viking is the most binary of the three. Its injectable obesity drug VK2735 is in two fully enrolled Phase 3 trials with top-line data in the second half of 2027, and an oral version has just entered Phase 3. The prize is large, but the market is dominated by two well-funded incumbents and prices are falling.

What this means for investors

We would rank the three by risk rather than by upside. Incyte offers biotech exposure backed by earnings, with the 2028 Jakafi expiry as the main debate. Immunovant is a pre-revenue story whose valuation already reflects part of the good news. Viking remains binary until its 2027 Phase 3 data.
