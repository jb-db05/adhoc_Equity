1. What the company does

Immunovant develops IMVT-1402, an antibody that blocks the FcRn receptor and so lowers the level of IgG antibodies that drive many autoimmune diseases. The company has stopped developing its first-generation drug, batoclimab, to concentrate fully on IMVT-1402.

2. Why it could do well

In November the 600 mg dose met the primary endpoint of a randomised withdrawal study in difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis: 92.5% of patients kept their ACR20 response at week 28, against 75.6% on placebo. This is the first sign that an FcRn blocker can work in a large, common disease, which would widen the market well beyond rare neuromuscular conditions. An End of Phase 2 meeting with the FDA is planned by year-end.

Registrational data in Graves' disease and myasthenia gravis are due in 2027, and in CIDP in 2028.

3. What sets it apart

IMVT-1402 is designed to reduce IgG deeply without the falls in albumin and rises in LDL cholesterol seen with earlier agents, and to be given as a simple injection. Graves' disease, where no FcRn drug is approved, could be a first-in-class opportunity.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not on any conventional measure. An enterprise value of about USD 6.4 bn for a company without revenue already assumes success in several indications. The share is better seen as a call on the breadth of the FcRn class. Cash should fund operations to a potential launch in Graves' disease, which limits near-term dilution risk.

5. Main risks

Competition is intense: established FcRn drugs from larger groups are already on the market in myasthenia gravis and CIDP. The arthritis result rests on one dose in a withdrawal design where the placebo arm also did well, and a second registrational trial will be needed, so an arthritis launch is several years away. The company has also dropped cutaneous lupus and is unlikely to pursue Sjögren's disease, a reminder that not every indication works. Roivant controls the majority of the shares, which limits the influence of minority holders.

6. What to watch next

The outcome of the FDA meeting and the design of the next arthritis trial, then the Graves' disease and myasthenia gravis readouts in 2027.

Conclusion

Immunovant has moved from a promise to a partly validated platform. The 2027 readouts could re-rate the share again, but much of the good news is already reflected in a USD 7 bn valuation. It could suit a satellite position for an investor who accepts pre-revenue risk and a long road to profits.
