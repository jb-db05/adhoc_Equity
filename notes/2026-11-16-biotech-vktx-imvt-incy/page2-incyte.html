1. What the company does

Incyte develops and sells medicines in haematology, oncology and dermatology. Jakafi (ruxolitinib), used in myelofibrosis, polycythaemia vera and graft-versus-host disease, is still its largest product. Opzelura, a ruxolitinib cream for eczema and vitiligo, is the second growth pillar.

2. Why it could do well

The business beyond Jakafi is growing fast. In the third quarter total revenue rose 14% to USD 1.56 bn and Opzelura sales rose 36% to USD 255 m. Management raised its full-year net sales guidance, and Jakafi XR, a once-daily formulation, has started to contribute.

The late-stage pipeline is the real lever: an antibody targeting mutant calreticulin (INCA033989) is in a pivotal study in essential thrombocythaemia, with a myelofibrosis study to follow, and a KRAS G12D inhibitor (INCB161734) is heading for Phase 3 data in first-line pancreatic cancer in 2028.

3. What sets it apart

Incyte funds its own pipeline from profits and still has a large cash balance after paying USD 1.25 bn upfront for Vega Therapeutics. That deal added latarcibart for von Willebrand disease and confirms that management is willing to buy growth.

4. Is the share cheap?

At about four times this year's expected net sales (Syz calculation, based on a Bloomberg enterprise value of USD 20.8 bn), Incyte trades at a modest multiple for a profitable biotech. The discount reflects the 2028 patent cliff: Jakafi still represents roughly 60% of net sales, and its US revenue could fall sharply after generic entry. The share looks inexpensive if the pipeline delivers, but not if it disappoints.

5. Main risks

A gap in revenue between the Jakafi expiry and the arrival of new products; setbacks in the mutCALR or KRAS programmes; pricing pressure, including the US Medicare drug price negotiation; and the risk of overpaying for further acquisitions. Third-quarter GAAP earnings were a loss because of a USD 1.23 bn one-off charge for the Vega acquisition, a reminder that deal costs weigh on reported profits.

6. What to watch next

The start of the Phase 3 myelofibrosis study by the end of 2026, further data on the mutCALR antibody, the uptake of Jakafi XR, and the 2027 guidance in early 2027.

Conclusion

Incyte is the most defensive of the three names. Its earnings and cash give it time to bridge the 2028 cliff, and the valuation already prices in a good part of the risk. The pipeline readouts of 2027 and 2028 will be the main tests.
