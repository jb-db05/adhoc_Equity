SpaceX: Starship is closer to full reuse, the spending is now the test

17 November 2026 · Syz Research
IMPORTANT NOTICE

Prices are as of the close on 12 November 2026. This note reviews SpaceX after the first recovery of a Starship upper stage and the third-quarter results. Figures come from company publications, Bloomberg and press reports; multiples are Syz calculations. This document is not a personalised recommendation.

From launch provider to AI landlord

The company has changed in two quarters

SpaceX listed on Nasdaq in June at USD 135 per share, raising about USD 85.7 bn, the largest IPO on record. Since then the business mix has turned over. In the third quarter the Super Intelligence segment, which rents AI computing capacity and sells software such as the Cursor coding tool, brought in USD 7.6 bn, or 52% of group revenue. Starlink connectivity added USD 5.2 bn (+69% year on year) and the historical launch business USD 1.8 bn.

Group revenue rose 161% to USD 14.6 bn, net income reached USD 4.4 bn and adjusted EBITDA USD 8.8 bn, a 60% margin. Management guides first-quarter 2027 revenue to USD 33 to 37 bn, more than double the third quarter.

Why the Starship catch matters

On 14 November the launch tower caught the Starship upper stage after about 22 hours and 14 orbits, during which it released 26 Starlink V3 satellites. Boosters have been caught since 2024, but this is the first time an orbital-class upper stage has come back intact. A fully reusable Starship would cut the cost of placing the much larger V3 satellites and, later, orbital computing hardware. The same flight shows the limits: the booster catch was called off and the booster was diverted to the sea.

The bill comes first

The AI buildout is extremely capital hungry. SpaceX spent USD 48.1 bn of capex in the first nine months of 2026 against USD 13.1 bn of operating cash flow, and guides USD 35 to 40 bn for the fourth quarter alone. Full-year capex would therefore reach roughly USD 85 bn. The gap has been funded by the IPO cash (USD 89.9 bn of cash and marketable securities at end-September) and, from 2027, by a USD 40 bn GPU finance lease with an Apollo-managed entity.

Management targets USD 100 bn of annual recurring revenue by December 2026. On the October call, Elon Musk went further and said recurring revenue could reach USD 500 bn to 1 tn by the end of 2027. That is a personal projection rather than guidance, and it depends on power, cooling and chips being delivered on time.

What this means for investors

SpaceX owns assets that no competitor could rebuild quickly: reusable rockets, the largest satellite constellation and, increasingly, AI capacity. Rivals are struggling: on 15 November ULA's Vulcan failed on its return to flight and lost 40 Amazon Leo satellites, and Blue Origin's New Glenn was destroyed on its pad in May. The share, however, trades close to its IPO price, at around USD 1.75 tn of market value. Investors are weighing strong growth against heavy spending, an early-December lock-up expiry and governance questions. We would build exposure gradually rather than in one go.
