Why the third quarter disappointed

Third-quarter revenue rose 59% year on year to CNY 165.2bn, but net profit rose only 14.5% to CNY 21.2bn, about 6% less than in the second quarter: the slowest profit growth in almost two years. The net margin fell to 12.9%, from 15.6% in the first half, and the gross margin to about 21%, from about 23% in the second quarter.

Four factors explain most of the gap: a larger share of storage systems, which include bought-in parts and earn a lower percentage margin; the first month of the new consumption tax; the first full quarter of the lower export VAT rebate; and impairments of about CNY 1.7bn, mostly on inventories after swings in lithium prices. Nine-month operating cash flow fell 23.6% to CNY 61.6bn, which management puts down to faster and advance payments to suppliers, not weaker demand.

Four growth engines beyond the passenger car

Energy storage

First worldwide in storage-battery shipments in the first half of 2026. CATL increasingly sells complete systems, such as its TENER range, with installation and service: more value per project, but a lower percentage margin.

Sodium-ion

A second chemistry that needs no lithium, suited to grid storage, low-cost cars and cold climates. An agreement signed in April 2026 covers 60 GWh of sodium-ion batteries for storage over three years. A milestone to verify, not yet a profit driver.

Battery swapping

Choco-swap stations for cars and swap stations for heavy trucks (305 at the end of 2025, 900 planned for 2026). Swapping could lower vehicle prices and bring recurring income, but stations only pay off if heavily used.

Plants abroad

Germany is producing, trial production started in Hungary in September 2026, and a 50 GWh joint venture with Stellantis is being built in Spain. Local plants ease trade friction but add execution and political risk.

What would strengthen our view
  • Customers agree to share the consumption tax and the end of the export rebate.
  • The gross margin holds near current levels in the fourth quarter despite the full tax.
  • Storage keeps growing fast without further margin dilution.
  • On-time ramp-up abroad, and the buyback carried out.
What would change our view
  • Lasting share loss in China as carmakers move to second suppliers.
  • The price war spreads to storage batteries.
  • New US or European restrictions on Chinese battery makers or their licences.
  • Cash flow stays weak after supplier payments normalise.
