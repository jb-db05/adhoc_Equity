1. What the company does

CATL (Contemporary Amperex Technology) is the world's largest battery maker. It supplies batteries for electric and hybrid cars, buses and trucks, and large systems storing electricity for grids, factories and data centres. In 2025 it sold 661 GWh of batteries, 39% more than a year earlier: 541 GWh for vehicles and 121 GWh for storage. The shares are listed in Shenzhen and, since May 2025, in Hong Kong.

2. Why it could do well

  • Still the leader, and still gaining. CATL supplied 40.2% of the world's electric-vehicle batteries from January to May 2026, 2.2 points more than a year earlier. It also ranked first in storage-battery shipments in the first half.
  • Storage is a second engine. Storage revenue rose 88% to CNY 53.3bn in the first half of 2026, about a fifth of sales, with a gross margin close to 24%. This business depends much less on carmakers.
  • Strong growth, plenty of cash. Revenue rose 56% to CNY 442bn in the first nine months of 2026. Operating cash flow reached CNY 133bn in 2025, and the company has proposed buying back CNY 20 to 40bn of its own shares.

3. What sets it apart

Its scale is hard to copy: 772 GWh of capacity at the end of 2025, another 321 GWh under construction, and CNY 22bn spent on research in 2025. The range covers cheaper LFP cells, high-performance nickel cells, fast-charging Shenxing and Qilin batteries and sodium-ion. CATL also owns lithium, nickel and phosphate resources, and builds plants in Germany, Hungary and Spain to serve European carmakers locally.

4. Cheap, or a value trap?

At CNY 227.90, the share trades at 11.2 times expected 2026 earnings, its lowest level since the 2018 listing on Bloomberg data, against an average of 19.4x since 2023. It has fallen 38% since the start of the year and is about half its May high, while expected 2026 earnings are still 6% higher than in January. We do not see a value trap: profits are growing and the balance sheet is strong. But estimates are being cut (−3.6% since April) and the fourth quarter bears the new tax in full, so the low multiple could last.

5. Main risks

  • Margins. Customers have not yet agreed to share the new consumption tax. Management said fourth-quarter profit per unit could look like the third quarter's if this is not resolved.
  • Customers diversifying. Carmakers under pressure add second suppliers to win better prices.
  • Geopolitics. The US market is largely closed and Europe could tighten its rules; new overseas plants add execution risk.
  • Cash flow. Operating cash flow fell 24% in the first nine months as CATL paid suppliers faster and in advance.

6. What to watch next

Agreements with customers on sharing the tax, the fourth-quarter margin with the annual results, the ramp-up in Hungary, the execution of the buyback, and storage and sodium-ion deliveries.

Conclusion

CATL is a high-quality compounder caught in a tax- and price-driven earnings reset, not a structurally impaired business. We are positive over two to three years but cautious over the next few quarters: the valuation reflects a lot of bad news, yet the market would likely need proof that margins are stabilising before paying more. For a long-term investor, a gradual approach would suit this mix better than a single entry.
