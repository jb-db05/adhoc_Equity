CATL: the battery leader, caught in an earnings reset

21 October 2026 · Syz Research

Important notice. We do not cover CATL and are not promoting it. This note is an ad-hoc analysis at request. It relies on company publications, Bloomberg data and public information. It contains no house price target and does not constitute a recommendation to buy or sell. Prices are as of the close on 21 October 2026 (Shenzhen-listed A-share, 300750 CH).

The big picture: battery demand is strong, the profit pool is being shared

China's economy runs at two speeds. Exports rose 25% year on year in August and advanced manufacturing is growing fast, while retail sales barely grow and the credit impulse, at −4.1, is still negative. Batteries belong to the strong side: they are one of the country's leading export and technology industries.

Demand is not the problem. Global electric-vehicle battery usage rose 16% in the first five months of 2026, and seven Chinese makers now hold almost three quarters of the market. In September, Chinese battery sales rose 52% year on year and storage batteries more than doubled (+104%). Storage, driven by solar and wind power and by data centres for artificial intelligence, now makes up about a third of Chinese battery sales.

Carmakers, by contrast, are squeezed by a long price war at home. They push their suppliers for lower prices and add second suppliers to gain bargaining power.

Beijing is also taking a bigger share. The export VAT rebate on batteries was cut from 9% to 6% in April 2026 and will disappear on 1 January 2027. A 2% consumption tax on lithium batteries has applied since 1 September 2026 and doubles to 4% in September 2027.

Trade remains a constraint. The US market is largely closed to Chinese battery suppliers, and Europe charges extra duties on Chinese electric cars, which pushes battery makers to build plants locally.

What this means for investors. We remain cautious on the broad Chinese market but favour selected leaders in advanced manufacturing. In batteries, the potential winners are the companies with the scale, technology and balance sheet to absorb taxes and price pressure. The question is less how fast volumes grow than how much of that growth reaches shareholders.

CATL in three points
  • Long term positive. CATL remains the strongest battery franchise in the world, with scale, technology, customer trust and a balance sheet that rivals struggle to match.
  • Near term cautious. Third-quarter profit grew far less than sales. New taxes, tougher customers and a lower-margin product mix weigh on profit per battery sold, and the fourth quarter carries the new tax in full.
  • Cheap, not yet free of risk. At 11.2 times expected 2026 earnings, the share is at its lowest valuation since its 2018 listing, but earnings estimates are still being cut.
