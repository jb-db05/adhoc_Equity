The share price is just above its 40-day average (EUR 90.2) and below its 200-day average (EUR 95.5), after a 27% fall from August to October and a partial recovery. A close above EUR 95.8, the 11 November high, could confirm the improvement; EUR 82.6, the low of 22 October, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

BNP Paribas is France's largest bank and one of the largest in Europe. It combines Corporate and Institutional Banking (investment banking, markets and financing), Commercial, Personal Banking and Services (retail banks in France, Belgium, Italy and Luxembourg, plus Arval and leasing) and Investment and Protection Services (asset management, insurance and wealth).

2. Why it could do well

Earnings are growing across divisions, with third-quarter net income up 13.5%. Investment banking is gaining ground, particularly in equities. The bank plans to double pre-tax income in asset management by 2030 and to lift returns at its Italian arm BNL. A EUR 1.3bn buyback is under way and the board is considering a payout of 70% of earnings instead of 60%.

3. What sets it apart

Exposure to France is lower than the label suggests: more than 70% of loans are outside the country, and French government debt is under 1% of the balance sheet. S&P sees BNP Paribas as the only large French bank that could be rated above the state, and Fitch already does (AA- against A+). The bank is also far stronger than in 2008 or during the euro-area debt crisis: a CET1 ratio of about 13%, 2.5 points above the minimum, against a Tier 1 ratio of 7.8% at the end of 2008.

4. Is the share cheap?

Against its own history, modestly so. The shares trade on about 7.1 times consensus earnings for the next twelve months, against an average of 8.2 times since 2005 and 7.7 times over ten years. Earnings estimates have risen 15% since the end of last year and the multiple has not followed: the discount reflects the French label more than the results.

5. Main risks

S&P's decision on France, and litigation that can make headlines: the Sudan case (a US jury found the bank liable in October 2025 and awarded about USD 21m to three plaintiffs; the bank is appealing, and plaintiffs' lawyers say it could open the way to many more claims), the Madoff-related claims, and Swiss franc loans in Poland, largely provisioned. French politics and weaker euro-area growth could also affect lending, and investment banking revenues can fall when markets turn.

6. What to watch next

S&P's decision on France; the Group 2030 plan, expected early in 2027; the buyback and any increase in the payout; the sale of the Moroccan subsidiary BMCI; the Sudan appeal.

CONCLUSION

BNP Paribas is delivering growing profits, a strong capital position and rising shareholder returns, yet its shares sit 19% below their August high and below their average multiple. The gap reflects caution on French politics and legal risk rather than weaker results. The strategic plan expected in early 2027 and the pace of buybacks are the next tests of whether that caution eases.
