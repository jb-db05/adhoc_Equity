We like European banks; the French label is what holds BNP Paribas back

A good quarter, an unloved label

We like European banks. What we like less about BNP Paribas is the French label: the share is penalised by French political risk more than by its results. For now it stays on our Focus List.

The third quarter was good. Net income rose 13.5% to EUR 3.46bn, ahead of expectations, with equities trading revenue up about 50% and pre-tax profit in Corporate and Institutional Banking up 35% to EUR 2.25bn. A EUR 1.3bn share buyback is under way, and the board is considering distributing 70% of earnings instead of 60%.

The share price has not followed. After reaching EUR 112.80 on 10 August, the shares fell about 27% to EUR 82.57 on 22 October. At EUR 91.00 they are 19% below the August high.

Less exposed to France than it looks

More than 70% of the bank's loans are outside France. S&P considers BNP Paribas the only large French bank that could be rated above the state, and Fitch already rates it above (AA- against A+). French government debt is under 1% of the balance sheet and does not have to be marked to market while it is held to maturity.

What this means for investors

The shares trade on about 7 times expected earnings, against an average of 8.2 times since 2005, while earnings estimates and capital returns are rising. The next data points are S&P's decision on France, due today, the Group 2030 plan expected early in 2027, and the progress of the buyback.
