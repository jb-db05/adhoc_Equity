The share price sits between its 40-day (EUR 170.9) and 200-day (EUR 176.4) averages, after giving back part of the rally that followed the results. A close above EUR 193.8, the high of 6 November, could confirm the recovery; EUR 150.5, the close before the results, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

EssilorLuxottica designs, makes and sells prescription lenses (Essilor, Varilux, Transitions), frames and sunglasses (Ray-Ban, Oakley and licensed brands) and operates retail banners such as LensCrafters and Sunglass Hut. Its Eyecare and Medtech activities now also include myopia management, hearing glasses and ophthalmic equipment.

2. Why it could do well

Third-quarter revenue grew 7.2% at constant exchange rates, with Asia-Pacific up 15.7%. Stellest, now in about 12,000 US doors, grew 24%, and the wearables business grew 20% to EUR 486m. A smart frame can also carry prescription and light-adaptive lenses, which could add lens value to each pair sold.

3. What sets it apart

Owning lenses, frames, brands and stores lets the group sell premium products directly and collect the margin at each step. Its partnership with Meta has created a new category, and the agreement with Applied Materials aims to bring in-house expertise in waveguides and adaptive lenses for future glasses.

4. Is the share cheap?

Cheaper than its own history. At EUR 172.15 the shares trade on 22.1 times consensus earnings for the next twelve months, against 27.1 times on average over five years and 27.5 times over ten. Over the past five years the multiple has been lower on only about 8% of trading days. Earnings estimates are almost unchanged (EUR 7.80, against EUR 7.94 at the start of the year), so the lower share price reflects a lower multiple. A low multiple can stay low if the market doubts the margins of new products.

5. Main risks

Wearables could remain less profitable than the core business while volumes grow. The category depends on one partner, Meta, and could draw more competition and privacy scrutiny. Weaker consumer spending, notably in the United States, could slow store traffic and push buyers towards cheaper lenses. Currency moves also matter: reported growth of 9% compares with 7.2% at constant rates.

6. What to watch next

Full-year results and any margin commentary; whether wearables growth stabilises after the slowdown from the second quarter; the pace of the Stellest roll-out in the United States; progress on the Applied Materials programme and on future smart glasses.

CONCLUSION

EssilorLuxottica reported solid third-quarter growth, with Asia-Pacific, myopia management and wearables ahead of the core. Earnings expectations have held up, while the share price is 36% lower than at the start of the year and the multiple is below its five and ten-year averages. The next phase depends on whether wearables can grow while keeping acceptable margins, and on the continued rise of Stellest. Concentration on one partner and the consumer backdrop are the main risks.
