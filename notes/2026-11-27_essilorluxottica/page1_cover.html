Results that beat expectations, a valuation that has not followed

Growth in the core and in new categories

EssilorLuxottica owns the whole chain, from lenses and frames to retail banners such as LensCrafters and Sunglass Hut. In the third quarter, revenue rose 9% to EUR 7.49bn, or 7.2% at constant exchange rates, ahead of analyst forecasts. Eyecare and Medtech grew 6.5%, comparable sales in the group's own stores rose 6.8% and Asia-Pacific led the regions with +15.7%.

Two newer categories add to this base. Wearables, which groups AI glasses and hearing glasses, reached EUR 486m, up 20%. The myopia management portfolio, led by Stellest lenses for children, grew 24% in the quarter and over nine months. Stellest is now distributed in about 12,000 doors in the United States, following its FDA authorisation in September 2025.

In June 2026 the group also signed a long-term joint development agreement with Applied Materials, focused on waveguides and adaptive lens systems for future smart glasses.

A share price that has only partly recovered

The shares jumped 15.5% on 21 October, from EUR 150.50 to EUR 173.80, and reached EUR 193.80 on 6 November. They have since given back part of the move and closed at EUR 172.15, 36% below the end of 2025 and 39% below the January high of EUR 282. The low of the year, EUR 139.00, came on 18 September.

Wearables growth of 20% is a slowdown from the second quarter, when the category almost doubled, and investors are weighing how profitable smart glasses will be while volumes build.

What this means for investors

Expected earnings have barely moved: consensus for the next twelve months is EUR 7.80 per share, against EUR 7.87 a year ago. The fall in the share price has therefore come from a lower multiple. The shares trade on 22.1 times expected earnings, against averages of 27.1 times over five years and 27.5 times over ten. Wearables growth, the pace of the Stellest roll-out and the margin commentary at the next results are the data points to follow.
