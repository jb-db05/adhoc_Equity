The market the group works in

EssilorLuxottica is the world's largest eyewear company, with revenue of EUR 28.5bn in 2025. It makes lenses (Essilor, Varilux, Transitions, Stellest), designs and distributes frames and sunglasses (Ray-Ban and Oakley, which it owns, and licensed brands such as Prada, Chanel and Armani) and sells to the public through its own stores and online (Sunglass Hut, LensCrafters, GrandVision).

Demand is steady and largely non-discretionary. Glasses are a medical need for a large share of the world's population, populations are ageing and myopia among children is rising fast: a widely cited projection is that about half of the world's population could be short-sighted by 2050. Few competitors can match the group's scale, which is why the shares have long been seen as a defensive holding.

Three growth areas sit on top of this base. Myopia management lenses such as Stellest grew 24% in the third quarter. Hearing glasses (Nuance Audio) take the group into hearing care, where more than a billion people live with some hearing loss. And AI glasses, built with Meta, are the largest and the most debated of the three.

Why the share price has fallen so far

The shares closed at EUR 172.15, 36% below their level at the start of 2026 and 46% below the record close of EUR 320.7 in November 2025. Expected earnings per share for the next twelve months barely moved over the year, from EUR 7.87 to EUR 7.80. The whole fall comes from the multiple, which went from about 40 times earnings to 22 times.

The market appears to have stopped paying a premium for the smart-glasses story. The ramp-up weighed on margins (the adjusted operating margin fell 70 basis points to 16.0% in 2025, with US tariffs also a factor), competition from larger technology groups became easier to imagine, and privacy concerns grew.

The third-quarter release of 20 October was the turning point: sales rose 7.2% at constant exchange rates to EUR 7,486m, above analysts' expectations, and the shares rose 15.5% the next day, from EUR 150.5 to EUR 173.8.

What this means for investors

The lens, frame and retail business gives the group a stable base, and the shares now trade on about 22 times expected earnings against a ten-year average of 27.5 times. How much the market will pay for the smart-glasses story is the open question, and it depends on adoption, competition and public acceptance.
