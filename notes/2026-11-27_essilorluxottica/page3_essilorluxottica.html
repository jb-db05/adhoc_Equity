The shares stand just below their 200-day average (EUR 176.4) and above the 40-day average (EUR 170.9). A close above EUR 194, the 6 November high, could confirm a change of trend; EUR 150, the level before the results, and EUR 139, the September low, are the levels to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

EssilorLuxottica was formed by the 2018 merger of Essilor (lenses) and Luxottica (frames and retail), and added GrandVision's stores in 2021. Revenue in 2025 was EUR 28.5bn, up 7.5% (11.2% at constant exchange rates). It sells lenses and frames to opticians and, increasingly, directly to consumers in its own stores and online. Wearables, myopia management and hearing glasses are the growth areas.

2. Why it could do well

Third-quarter revenue rose 7.2% at constant exchange rates, with Asia-Pacific up 15.7%, EMEA up 7.5% and North America up 4.7%. In the first half, revenue grew 9.7% and adjusted operating profit 15%, at a margin of 18.6%. Myopia management grew 24% in the third quarter and wearables 20%, with the products shown in September still to contribute. Much of the business recurs: people replace lenses and frames regularly and need them for medical reasons.

3. What sets it apart

No competitor combines lenses, frames, brands and retail at this scale. Ray-Ban and Oakley are among the best-known eyewear brands. The medtech side includes Stellest for myopia, Nuance Audio for hearing (a second generation launched this autumn) and, with Meta, the largest position in AI glasses.

4. Is the share cheap?

Cheaper than it has been for most of the past decade. The shares trade on 22.1 times expected earnings, against 27.5 over ten years and about 40 a year ago, while the earnings forecast has hardly changed (EUR 7.80 against EUR 7.87). The forward multiple touched 17.7x on 18 September, its lowest in ten years. A lower multiple is not the same as cheap: part of the earlier premium paid for smart-glasses growth, and the ramp-up has diluted margins.

5. Main risks

Dependence on Meta and its product roadmap. Competition from Google, Samsung, Apple and Chinese makers as the market grows. Privacy rules and retailer reactions in Europe. Margin dilution while smart glasses scale. US tariffs and currency moves, with North America at about 43% of third-quarter sales. A weaker consumer for licensed and sun brands.

6. What to watch next

Full-year 2026 results, due early in 2027, with the wearables margin; holiday sales of the new Ray-Ban Meta models and the rollout of Muse on the glasses; regulatory steps on camera glasses in Europe and whether more retailers follow Hans Anders; products from Google, Samsung and Apple; any change in Meta's stake or in the partnership.

CONCLUSION

EssilorLuxottica combines a stable lens, frame and retail business with a fast-growing but contested smart-glasses line. Over the past year its earnings forecasts have hardly moved while its multiple has fallen from about 40 to 22 times, which shows how much of the share price rested on sentiment around Meta glasses. The outcome could depend on adoption, on competition and, more and more, on the social and regulatory acceptance of camera glasses.
