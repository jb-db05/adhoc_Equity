Mayr-Melnhof: the cash is priced, the rest comes cheap

11 November 2026 · Syz Research
Important notice
Prices are as of the close on 11 November 2026. The special distribution is planned, not approved: its amount, timing and form depend on the closing of the transaction, expected in H1 2027.

A deal that splits the group in two

On 9 November, Mayr-Melnhof (MM) agreed to sell 70% of its Packaging business to the US private equity firm KPS Capital Partners and announced a special distribution to shareholders. The share rose 11% over the week to EUR 74.10, still close to its 23 October low (EUR 64.10), the lowest since at least 2014. Does the market price the distribution? It prices the cash. What it barely prices is the business that remains.

Packaging: the part a buyer wanted

Folding cartons for food, consumer brands and pharma sell on service, design and proximity to the customer, and demand follows consumption rather than the paper cycle. The business averaged an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 12% over 2021 to 2025, and in the first nine months of 2026 both packaging divisions improved their earnings on a like-for-like basis. At EUR 2.4bn, Packaging alone is now worth more than the whole group was before the deal (EUR 2.3bn enterprise value on 6 November). The group traded at about 6x EBITDA; Packaging is being sold at about 9x.

Board & Paper: the part the market ignores

Cartonboard and kraft paper are a commodity business with heavy fixed costs, in a Europe with too much capacity and high energy and fibre costs. Over nine months its adjusted EBITDA more than halved to EUR 39m and it lost EUR 44m at operating level, despite slightly higher volumes. After closing, it becomes MM's core business, with a long-term contract to keep supplying the Packaging business it just sold. A mill with high fixed costs gains a lot of profit from a small rise in prices, so the upside is real when the cycle turns, but nobody can date that turn today.

What this means for investors
  • The special distribution is real, but it is not free money: it is cash that will already sit on the balance sheet, and the share price would fall by the same amount on the ex-date.
  • What the market does not price is what remains after the cash and the stake: at EUR 74.10, Board & Paper is valued at about EUR 0.23bn, less than 2x its 2025 EBITDA (page 2).
  • The case suits investors comfortable with a family-owned small cap and a 2027 horizon: the free float is 42%, about EUR 2.2m trades a day, and the amount, timing and form of the payout are not locked.
