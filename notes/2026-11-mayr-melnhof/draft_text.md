### Two businesses, two very different markets (two columns)

**Packaging: the part a buyer wanted.**
Folding cartons for food, consumer brands and pharma sell on service, design and proximity to the customer. Margins are steadier, demand follows consumption rather than the paper cycle, and the pharma part keeps growing. In the first nine months of 2026, Food & Premium and Pharma & Healthcare Packaging both improved their adjusted EBITDA on a like-for-like basis. That is exactly the type of asset private equity pays for, and 9x EBITDA is a full price for it: it crystallises value the market was not paying for inside a group dominated by a cyclical mill business.

**Board & Paper: the part the market is ignoring.**
Cartonboard and kraft paper are a commodity business with heavy fixed costs. Europe has too much capacity, energy and fibre costs stay high, and customers have been destocking. Board & Paper is the main reason group operating profit fell 39% to EUR 93m over nine months, even with slightly higher volumes and utilisation. After closing, it becomes MM's core business and an independent supplier to the packaging industry, including the Packaging business it just sold. The upside is real when the cycle turns, because a mill with high fixed costs gains a lot of profit from a small rise in prices, but nobody can date that turn today.

### A family company, with the trade-offs that come with it (short note)

The founding families hold about 58% of the shares through a syndicate; the rest is free float. In our experience, family control cuts both ways for an outside shareholder. On the positive side, it brings a long horizon, a conservative balance sheet and a habit of returning cash, and here the incentives are aligned: the families receive 58% of any special distribution, which makes it more likely to happen. On the other side, there is no takeover premium to expect, minority shareholders have little influence on the use of the remaining cash (MM has flagged acquisitions as well as debt repayment), and a 42% free float on a EUR 1.5bn company limits liquidity.

### What this means for investors

- The special distribution is real, but it is not free money: it is cash that will already be on the balance sheet, and the share price would fall by the same amount on the ex-date.
- What the market does not price is the business left after the cash and the stake: at EUR 74.10, Board & Paper is valued at about EUR 0.2 to 0.25bn (Syz calculation, page 2).
- This is a case for investors with an appetite for family-controlled small caps and the patience to wait until 2027: liquidity is limited, and the amount and timing of the payout are not locked.

## Page 2 · Company fiche

**Mayr-Melnhof Karton AG (MMK AV)**
*Orange subtitle:* A cash box with a cyclical mill attached, priced as if the mill were worth almost nothing

**KPI tiles**
- Share price: EUR 74.10 (+10.9% on the week)
- Market cap: about EUR 1.48bn (20m shares) [CHECK: share count net of treasury shares]
- Planned special distribution: EUR 25 to 30 per share
- Forward P/E: 9.8x vs. a 10-year average of 12.4x

**1. What the company does**
MM is Europe's largest producer of recycled cartonboard and one of the largest folding carton makers. After the KPS deal, it becomes a board and paper producer with net cash and a 30% stake in its former Packaging business.

**2. Why it could do well**
The deal turns a complex conglomerate into something simple: cash, a minority stake valued by a private equity buyer, and a mill business. Simple stories tend to get re-rated. The special distribution gives a visible return of 34 to 40% of today's price, and the remaining net cash leaves room for buybacks or a second payout if acquisitions do not materialise.

**3. What sets it apart**
The price tag on Packaging comes from a real transaction, not from an analyst's model, and MM keeps 30% of the upside if KPS grows the business. Few European small caps offer a balance sheet that goes from leveraged to net cash in one step.

**4. Is the share cheap? (Syz calculation)**

| Per share, EUR | Low | Mid | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro forma net cash (EUR 0.8 to 0.9bn / 20m) | 40.0 | 42.5 | 45.0 |
| 30% Packaging stake at the deal value (EUR 0.4bn) | 20.0 | 20.0 | 20.0 |
| Cash and stake | 60.0 | 62.5 | 65.0 |
| Share price | 74.10 | 74.10 | 74.10 |
| Implied value of Board & Paper | 14.1 | 11.6 | 9.1 |
| In EUR bn | 0.28 | 0.23 | 0.18 |

Method: net cash guided by MM after closing plus the 30% stake at the price MM is reinvesting, divided by 20m shares; the remainder is what the market pays for Board & Paper. Cash and stake explain 84% of the share price. The stock trades at 9.8x forward earnings vs. a 10-year average of 12.4x, and is still 60% below its January 2022 peak (EUR 186).
The deal presentation confirms the net cash is measured before the special distribution (EUR 0.5 to 0.6bn paid out, about EUR 0.3bn retained), so the calculation holds. On 2025 adjusted EBITDA of EUR 138m (Board & Paper and remaining packaging operations), EUR 0.23bn is less than 2x EBITDA, for a business that averaged a 9% EBITDA margin over 2021 to 2025.

The EUR 0.4bn for 30% is not 30% of EUR 2.4bn (EUR 0.72bn): it implies about EUR 1.3bn of equity for 100%, so the new holding would carry about EUR 1.0 to 1.1bn of debt (Syz calculation). MM's stake is leveraged equity: more upside if KPS executes, more downside if it does not.

**5. Main risks**
- Amount, timing and form of the distribution are not locked: approvals, the tax bill on the disposal and M&A ambitions can all reduce it.
- The stake can fall to 20% if Packaging misses its 2027 targets, worth about EUR 7 per share.
- Board & Paper earnings are under pressure and could weigh on the group for longer; the cash could be spent on acquisitions instead of returned.
- Closing risk: regulatory approvals in H1 2027.
- Liquidity: family control, a 42% free float and a small market cap mean wide spreads and difficult exits in stressed markets [ADD: average daily traded value from Bloomberg].

**6. What to watch next**
- Confirmation of the form of the payout (dividend or capital repayment) and of the net cash definition.
- Signs of a price recovery in European cartonboard.
- The capital allocation policy for the remaining cash.

**Swiss investors: the form of the payout matters.** A dividend bears 27.5% Austrian withholding tax, of which 12.5 points can be reclaimed under the Austria–Switzerland tax treaty, leaving 15%. A repayment of capital from capital reserves would in principle not bear withholding tax [CHECK with Tax]. Management says the form is still open.

**Conclusion**
The special distribution is not what makes the case; it only returns cash the market already counts. The case is that, after the cash and the stake, an investor buys a European board and paper business for about EUR 0.2bn at the bottom of its cycle. It suits investors who are comfortable with a family-controlled small cap, limited liquidity and a 2027 horizon, and who see a cyclical recovery in board prices as an option rather than a certainty.
