Imperial Brands has started its second five-year plan (FY2026 to FY2030) as a self-described challenger: it protects value rather than volume in cigarettes and is building scale in next-generation products. Full-year FY2026 results are due on 17 November 2026.

Tobacco: a shrinking market that still generates cash

Pricing still outruns volume

Cigarette volumes keep falling in developed markets, but price increases have so far more than offset the decline for the large players. Imperial Brands expects FY2026 to be its sixth consecutive year of tobacco net revenue growth, helped by pricing and share gains in the United States and Germany. The other side of the coin is taxation: excise duties rise regularly, and further increases are planned in Germany, one of the group's key markets.

The race for next-generation products

Growth in the industry now comes from vapour, heated tobacco and modern oral nicotine pouches. These categories are led by larger groups with bigger budgets, so smaller players have to choose their battles. Imperial Brands focuses on a limited number of markets and buys brands to move faster, such as Black Buffalo in the United States and Helwit in Sweden, both in modern oral nicotine.

What this means for investors

For an investor, tobacco is mainly a source of cash returns rather than growth. The key question for the sector is whether next-generation products can grow fast enough to replace the profit lost on cigarettes. For Imperial Brands, the case rests first on free cash flow and buybacks, with next-generation products as a possible upside.
