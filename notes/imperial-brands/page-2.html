1. What the company does

Imperial Brands is a UK-listed tobacco group. Most of its profit comes from combustible tobacco in five priority markets, including the United States and Germany. Its next-generation products cover vapour (blu), heated tobacco (Pulze 3.0) and modern oral nicotine (Zone, Skruf, Helwit).

2. Why it could do well

Pricing power remains strong: tobacco net revenue should grow for a sixth year in a row. For FY2026, management guides for adjusted operating profit growth of 3 to 5% and high-single-digit growth in adjusted earnings per share, helped by buybacks. Next-generation products are expected to grow at a double-digit rate, with share gains in all three categories.

3. What sets it apart

The group openly puts value before volume and accepts losing some market share where volumes are not profitable. Its acquisitions are small and targeted: Black Buffalo in May 2026, for an initial USD 150m, and Yoik Group, owner of Helwit, in September 2026. This leaves most of the cash available for shareholders.

4. Is the share cheap?

Free cash flow of more than GBP 2.2bn represents about 10% of the market value of GBP 21.6bn. The GBP 1.5bn buyback planned for FY2027 alone equals about 7% of the market value, after GBP 1.45bn completed in FY2026. Leverage is moderate at 1.9 times EBITDA. The market prices a business in slow decline; the high cash yield is the compensation.

5. Main risks

Higher excise duties in Germany could weigh on volumes and pricing. Next-generation products lack scale against larger competitors, and the division grew only 7.5% in the first half of FY2026, with a decline in the United States. A faster fall in cigarette volumes would test the value-over-volume strategy.

6. What to watch next

The FY2026 results on 17 November 2026: whether next-generation products reached double-digit growth and free cash flow exceeded GBP 2.2bn. Then the pace of the FY2027 buyback, the German excise changes and the trend in the US next-generation business.

Conclusion

Imperial Brands could suit an investor looking for income and buybacks from a defensive sector. The share offers a high cash yield but limited growth, as the next-generation business is not yet large enough to change the picture. The FY2026 results will show whether the five-year plan is on track.
