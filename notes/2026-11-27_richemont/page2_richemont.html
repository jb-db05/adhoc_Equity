The share price is just above its 40-day (CHF 171.3) and 200-day (CHF 169.3) averages, after a rise from the March low and a pullback from the August high. A close above CHF 177, the 19 November high, could confirm the improvement; CHF 161.5, the low of 23 October, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

Richemont is a Swiss luxury group with three businesses: Jewellery Maisons (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Buccellati, Vhernier), Specialist Watchmakers (IWC, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Piaget, Vacheron Constantin, Panerai, A. Lange & Söhne) and Fashion & Accessories (Chloé, Montblanc, Dunhill, among others). It reports in euros and its shares are listed in Zurich. Jewellery Maisons represented EUR 9.1bn of EUR 12.3bn in first-half sales.

2. Why it could do well

Jewellery holds up better than fashion and leather goods, and the Maisons keep growing faster than the sector: +19% at constant rates in the half. Chinese customers now buy mostly in Hong Kong and Macao, a trend that favours jewellery over bags, and Greater China grew 7% in the half. Margins are rising despite the high gold price, and watches are recovering.

3. What sets it apart

Leadership in branded jewellery, with Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels, plus a strong position in high-end watches. Pricing is a real advantage: the group raised prices with moderation, growth comes from volumes and its brands are protected. The balance sheet is in a net cash position of EUR 7.7bn at the end of September.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not particularly. The shares trade on 22.8 times expected earnings for the next twelve months, against a ten-year average of 23.4 times, a five-year average of 21.5 times and a two-year average of 24.5 times. Expected earnings per share are 23% above their level at the end of 2025 while the share price has barely moved, so the multiple has fallen from 27.5 to 22.8 times. Other luxury groups have de-rated more, which makes Richemont look expensive by comparison and leaves little room for disappointment.

5. Main risks

The next quarters will be less easy, after an exceptional start to the year: jewellery growth already slowed to 14% in the second quarter. Gold remains expensive, even if the spot price has fallen, and weighs on gross margins. Sales in the Middle East fell about 2% in the half, and mainland China remains soft. A normalisation of jewellery demand, or a slowdown in watches, would weigh on a valuation that already assumes good execution.

6. What to watch next

The next trading update and the full-year results in May 2027; the pace of jewellery growth after the 14% of the second quarter; the price of gold and its effect on margins; the evolution of watches; sales in the Middle East and in Greater China.

CONCLUSION

Richemont is on the right side of luxury: jewellery holds up better than fashion, pricing has been measured and margins are rising. The results first disappointed because jewellery growth slowed to 14% after an exceptional start to the year, and the shares have gone sideways while earnings estimates have risen. The multiple is in line with its history, so further gains would likely depend on earnings delivery in tougher quarters.
