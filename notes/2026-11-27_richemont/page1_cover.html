Strong results, a share price that stayed put

The right side of the sector

Richemont is on the right side of the luxury sector. Jewellery (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels) is holding up better than fashion and leather goods, and Chinese customers now buy mostly in Hong Kong and Macao, which helps jewellery more than bags. Jewellery Maisons sales rose 19% at constant exchange rates to EUR 9.1bn in the first half of the fiscal year, about three quarters of group sales.

The results published on 13 November first disappointed. Jewellery growth slowed to 14% after an exceptional start to the year, and the shares fell about 4% on the day. Behind the headline, group sales rose 16% to EUR 12.3bn and operating profit rose by about a third to EUR 3.1bn: margins are improving despite the high price of gold, and watches are picking up.

The share price has gone sideways. After a high of CHF 200.80 on 10 August, the shares are at CHF 173.30, 14% lower and barely changed since the start of the year, while expected earnings per share are 23% higher.

Pricing is a real advantage

The group has raised prices with moderation. Its growth comes from volumes, and its brands come out of the exercise protected rather than stretched. This matters at a time when other luxury names are being questioned on pricing.

What this means for investors

The shares trade on 22.8 times expected earnings, in line with their ten-year average of 23.4 times, while other luxury groups have de-rated this year. The next quarters will be less easy: comparisons are tougher and gold remains expensive, even if the spot price has come down. A valuation in line with its history leaves little room for disappointment, as the reaction to the results showed. The next trading update and the full-year results in May are the next data points.
