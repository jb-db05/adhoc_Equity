Prudential now focuses entirely on Asia and Africa, where it holds top-three positions in many markets. A change in bank-channel rules in mainland China has slowed new business in 2026, while the rest of the group keeps growing at a double-digit rate.

Asian life insurance: long-term demand, short-term regulation

A large protection gap

Rising incomes and limited public safety nets in much of Asia support long-term demand for life, health and savings products. Insurers reach customers through two main channels: tied agents and banks (bancassurance). For Prudential, the bank channel produced USD 586m of new business profit in the first half of 2026, 42% of the group total, through more than 180 partner banks.

Regulators set the pace

In mainland China, new rules on expenses paid in the bancassurance channel have constrained sales in 2026. Prudential's joint venture, CITIC Prudential Life, saw new business profit fall sharply in the third quarter, also because of a high comparison base in July and August 2025. Elsewhere, repricing in Hong Kong has supported margins.

What this means for investors

Asian life insurance remains a structural growth story, but regulation can change the pace from one year to the next. For Prudential, the China setback is limited in size; the key question is whether growth outside China can keep the group on track for its 2027 targets.
