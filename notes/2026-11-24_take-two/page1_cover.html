A record launch, a share price that has not re-rated

A record that was largely expected

Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale on 19 November 2026 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after its predecessor. Take-Two estimates first-day retail sales at USD 3.79bn, with 32.5 million copies sold before launch and a Metacritic score of 99. On the company's figures, no film, album or game has ever opened bigger.

The market had priced much of this in. The shares rose about 22% between 30 October and 11 November, then gave back part of the gain around the launch. At USD 219.10 they are 14% below their level at the start of the year and 15% below the July high.

Two details matter for the numbers. The USD 3.79bn is a gross retail value: console makers, digital stores and retailers keep a share, so Take-Two's own net bookings will be lower. And the first full year of GTA VI sales is fiscal 2028, which starts in April 2027.

Where the debate is now

With the launch behind it, the question moves from whether the game works to how long it will pay. Grand Theft Auto V kept generating revenue for more than a decade after its 2013 release, mostly through its online mode. Investors will watch how online play around GTA VI is monetised, when a PC version arrives (no date has been given) and whether the rest of the pipeline reduces the dependence on one franchise.

The rest of the business is softer. In the September quarter net bookings fell 18% and recurrent consumer spending, mainly in-game purchases, fell 9%, worse than guided, because of NBA 2K.

What this means for investors

The launch removes the largest execution risk in the story, and the shares trade below their level of a year ago. On our numbers the enterprise value is about 5 times this year's guided net bookings, with the first full year of GTA VI still ahead. We are positive and would build positions gradually, using the volatility around sales updates rather than chasing rallies.
