The share price is back above its 40-day (USD 207.7) and 200-day (USD 218.6) averages, and trading volume roughly doubled in launch week. A close above USD 231, the 11 November high, could confirm the recovery; USD 190, the low of March and October, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

Take-Two publishes video games through Rockstar Games (Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead Redemption), 2K (NBA 2K, Borderlands, BioShock, Mafia) and Zynga in mobile. In fiscal 2026, to March, net bookings rose 19% to USD 6.72bn and recurrent consumer spending (in-game purchases, add-on content and in-game advertising) rose 16%.

2. Why it could do well

GTA VI sets a new revenue base: management guides fiscal 2027 net bookings of USD 8.0bn to 8.2bn, with the game contributing only four and a half months. A PC version and the online mode could extend sales for years, as they did for GTA V. Operating cash flow is guided above USD 1bn this year, and the company expects to reach net cash by March 2027.

3. What sets it apart

Few entertainment brands can sell tens of millions of copies before release. Rockstar publishes rarely, but each game becomes a platform that earns for a decade. NBA 2K brings steady yearly revenue, and a pipeline of 29 titles through fiscal 2029, including Judas and a new BioShock, offers some diversification.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not expensive against its own history. The shares trade on about 24 times consensus earnings for the next twelve months, against a ten-year average of about 28 times, and those estimates have risen about 7% since the end of October. On our numbers the market value is about USD 41bn and the enterprise value about USD 42bn, some 5 times fiscal 2027 guided net bookings. GAAP earnings say little this year: guided GAAP EPS of USD 0.57 to 0.75 reflects revenue deferral and amortisation.

5. Main risks

Concentration on Grand Theft Auto and NBA 2K. Weaker spending elsewhere, with recurrent consumer spending down 9% in the September quarter. Rising development and marketing budgets for large games. High expectations: any sign of slowing sales or a disappointing online offer could weigh on the shares. Leaks and piracy remain a risk for the whole industry.

6. What to watch next

Fiscal third-quarter results, with net bookings guided at USD 3.35bn to 3.50bn before the launch; the first sales updates after day one; dates for a PC version and for new online content; progress on the fiscal 2028 and 2029 pipeline.

CONCLUSION

Take-Two has delivered the launch the market was waiting for, and the first data point is a record. The shares have not re-rated: they trade below their level of a year ago and below their ten-year average multiple. The longer-term outcome depends on whether GTA VI becomes a multi-year platform as GTA V did, through its online mode and a future PC version. Concentration on a few franchises and high expectations are the main risks.
