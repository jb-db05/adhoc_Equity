Hermès: conclusion

RMS FP

The core has passed the test. The third quarter confirmed that Hermès' leather goods, close to half of sales, are a different business from the rest of luxury. They grew 11% at constant currency, above expectations, in a quarter when LVMH's fashion and leather goods fell 5% and Chinese demand weakened again. Demand still exceeds production, capacity keeps rising and the group has not needed discounts or volume to grow. Nothing in the quarter suggests that the scarcity model is weakening.

The second engine has not been spared. Ready-to-wear, silk, watches and the other Hermès sectors were flat to negative; management itself said the "volume divisions" are suffering. These businesses behave like the rest of the sector and are likely to recover only with it, when China, the wealth effect in the US and aspirational buyers improve. They explain why group growth slowed to 4.3%, below expectations and the 7% of the second quarter.

2027 looks like a year of transition. Smaller price increases, no major store openings and a stronger euro will weigh on growth, and earnings estimates have been lowered. The operating margin, at 41% in the first half, will be the measure of how well Hermès absorbs this.

The valuation now reflects the split. At 29.3 times expected profits, the share is at its lowest multiple since 2016, after a 41% fall in a year during which expected earnings rose. The market now values Hermès as half scarcity, half cyclical luxury. A rerating would probably need a recovery in the non-leather divisions or in China; until then, the leather business protects the earnings base, but not necessarily the multiple.

The chart has not turned yet. The share is in a downtrend below a falling 200-day average and made a new low at EUR 1,228.0 on 23 October. Momentum is deeply oversold, so a rebound towards EUR 1,500.0 could occur, but only a close above EUR 1,668.9 would end the downtrend.

What this means for an investor. The quarter answered the question of quality: the company's core is intact. The open question is time. The share may remain weak or range-bound until the luxury cycle turns, even though its most valuable business keeps growing. How to approach a position depends on an investor's time horizon, tolerance for waiting and the weight of Hermès and of luxury in the portfolio, including any holding in LVMH or Christian Dior, which share the same cycle. The relevant question is the potential from today's price. The next checkpoints are the full-year results in February 2027 (fourth-quarter growth, Asia excluding Japan and the 2027 margin) and leather growth holding above 8%.
