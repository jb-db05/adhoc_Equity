Possible approaches for a position in Hermès

The third quarter clarified the picture without providing a short-term catalyst. The leather engine is intact, but the cyclical divisions and China are not recovering yet, and 2027 starts with smaller price increases and no major store openings. The next checkpoint is the full-year results in February 2027. Each approach below is reasonable; the choice depends on an investor's time horizon, the weight of Hermès and of luxury in the portfolio, and tolerance for waiting.

1. Patience

Keep the position as it is and reassess at the full-year results in February 2027. The investor keeps full exposure to the leather engine, which the quarter showed intact, and to any recovery in the cyclical divisions, at the lowest multiple since 2016.

Trade-off: the share may not move for several quarters while the cyclical half and China weigh, and the 2027 headwinds are not yet visible in reported figures.

2. Reduce gradually

Lower the weight of the line in several steps rather than at once, for example on rebounds. The investor reduces concentration in a share whose trend remains weak, while keeping part of the exposure to the leather core and to a recovery.

Trade-off: if the cyclical divisions recover faster than expected, the investor participates less; if the share keeps drifting, rebounds to sell into may be limited.

3. Reallocate part of the position

Reinvest part of the line in other quality companies, inside or outside luxury, that depend less on China and on the luxury cycle. The investor diversifies the drivers of the portfolio while possibly keeping a smaller Hermès position.

Trade-off: this means selling at the lowest multiple in ten years and giving up part of the most resilient asset in the sector, with the risk of chasing names that have already risen. Building the new positions gradually could limit this risk.

In every case

The decision is best taken on the outlook from today's price rather than on the share's past path. An investor who also holds LVMH or Christian Dior should look at the combined luxury weight: Hermès' cyclical divisions move with LVMH's fashion and leather goods, so the exposures overlap. Leather growth below 8% for two quarters would be the clearest sign that the core is weakening.
