1. What the company does

Hermès runs two businesses under one name. Leather goods and saddlery (Birkin, Kelly and new models), about 47% of third-quarter sales, are made by hand in the group's own French workshops and sold with waiting lists. The rest of the house, from ready-to-wear and accessories (28% of sales) to silk, watches, perfumes and home, sells like other luxury goods.

2. Why it could do well

The scarcity engine is intact. Leather goods grew 11% in the third quarter, one point above consensus, driven by new models and icons. Demand still exceeds production.

Capacity keeps rising. New leather workshops open through 2030, adding about 6% of capacity a year; with price, this supports leather growth near 10%.

Growth outside China holds. The Americas grew 9%, Japan 10% and Europe 7%, and management said US growth recovered towards double digits at the end of September.

3. What sets it apart

Leather goods behave acyclically: they grew 11% while LVMH's fashion and leather goods fell 5%. Hermès grows its core without discounts or volume, with a 41% operating margin in the first half.

4. Is the share cheap?

Over 12 months the share fell 41% while expected earnings rose 3%: a derating, not an earnings problem. At 29.3 times expected profits, the lowest since April 2016, it trades 27% below its 40x average since 2005. How attractive 29x is depends on the cyclical half: valued like the sector, it would make today's multiple a fair blend; a recovery outside leather would make it look low.

5. Main risks

The second engine is suffering. Ready-to-wear and accessories fell 2%, silk rose only 1%, other Hermès sectors fell 1% and watches 6%. These categories move with the luxury cycle and are not recovering yet.

China. Asia excluding Japan, about 40% of sales, was flat after +3% in the second quarter; management linked China to equity markets and tax changes.

A tougher 2027. Price increases below the 5 to 6% of 2026, no major store openings after a 1 to 1.5 point contribution in 2026, and EUR 215m of currency headwind in 2026. Consensus earnings estimates were lowered after the release.

The share price trend remains weak. The share fell 4.9% on the release, then broke its September low of EUR 1,298.5 and touched EUR 1,228.0 on 23 October, its lowest since October 2022. It is at a one-year low relative to the Stoxx 600.

6. What to watch next

Full-year results in February 2027: fourth-quarter growth back above 6% with Asia excluding Japan positive again, and a 2027 operating margin held near 40%. Leather growth below 8% for two quarters would signal that demand no longer exceeds supply. The new Grace Wales Bonner menswear line starts in January 2027. On the charts, momentum is deeply oversold and a rebound towards EUR 1,500.0 could occur; EUR 1,162.0 is the level to watch on the downside, and only a close above the 200-day average (EUR 1,668.9) would end the downtrend.
