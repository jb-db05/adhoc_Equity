1. What the company does

Hermès runs two businesses. Leather goods and saddlery (Birkin, Kelly), about 47% of third-quarter sales, are made by hand in the group's own French workshops and sold with waiting lists. The rest of the house, from ready-to-wear and accessories (28% of sales) to silk, watches, perfumes and home, sells like other luxury goods.

2. Why it could do well

The scarcity engine is intact. Leather goods grew 11% in the third quarter, one point above consensus, driven by new models and icons. Demand still exceeds production.

Capacity keeps rising. New leather workshops open through 2030, adding about 6% of capacity a year; with price, this supports leather growth near 10%.

Growth outside China holds. The Americas grew 9%, Japan 10% and Europe 7%.

3. What sets it apart

Leather goods behave acyclically: they grew 11% while LVMH's fashion and leather goods fell 5%. Hermès grows its core without discounts or volume, with a 41% operating margin in the first half.

4. Is the share cheap?

Over 12 months the share fell more than 40% while expected earnings rose 3%: a derating, not an earnings problem. At 29.0 times expected profits, the lowest since April 2016, it trades more than a quarter below its 40x average since 2005. The cyclical half now sets the multiple.

5. Main risks

The second engine is suffering. Ready-to-wear and accessories fell 2%, silk rose only 1%, other Hermès sectors fell 1% and watches 6%. These categories move with the luxury cycle and are not recovering yet.

China. Asia excluding Japan, about 40% of sales, was flat after +3% in the second quarter.

A tougher 2027. Price increases below the 5 to 6% of 2026, no major store openings after a 1 to 1.5 point contribution in 2026, and EUR 215m of currency headwind in 2026.

The share price trend remains weak. The share fell 4.9% on the release, then broke its September low of EUR 1,298.5 and touched EUR 1,228.0 on 23 October, its lowest since October 2022.

6. What to watch next

Full-year results in February 2027: fourth-quarter growth back above 6% with Asia excluding Japan positive again, and a 2027 operating margin held near 40%. Leather growth below 8% for two quarters would signal that demand no longer exceeds supply. On the charts, the trend remains negative.

Conclusion

The third quarter confirmed that Hermès has two businesses. Leather goods, close to half of sales, proved their acyclical strength again, growing 11% while the sector's fashion and leather goods declined. The rest of the house, from ready-to-wear to watches, has suffered like the rest of luxury and is unlikely to recover before the sector does. The share now trades at its lowest multiple since 2016, which reflects this split rather than any doubt about the quality of the core. For an investor, the question is less the strength of the company than the time it may take for the cyclical half, and with it the share, to recover. The full-year results in February 2027 are the next checkpoint.
