Hermès after the third quarter: the leather core holds, the cyclical engine slows

26 October 2026 · CIO Office, Equity Team

Important notice. This note gives our opinion on Hermès after the third-quarter sales published on 22 October 2026. It relies on public information, company publications and third-party data. It contains no house price target and is not a personalised recommendation. Prices are as of the close on 22 October 2026; technical levels date from 26 October 2026.

The sector: luxury split in two, even inside Hermès

Luxury is in its third year of slowdown, and the recovery will be slow and uneven. European luxury groups had lost about €330bn of market value since January by mid-October. 2024 and 2025 were meant to be years of transition; 2026 is also turning out to be disappointing.

China no longer drives growth. The Chinese luxury market fell 3 to 5% in 2025, after a fall of 17 to 19% in 2024. Third-quarter sales confirmed it: LVMH again reported weak Chinese demand, and at Hermès, Asia excluding Japan, its largest region, was flat, which implies a slight decline in China.

The American client is still the main engine, but slowing. Hermès grew 9% in the Americas in the third quarter, against 15% in the first half. The risk is a stock market correction that would dent the wealth effect.

Price increases are no longer enough. Less wealthy "aspirational" buyers have dropped out, and Hermès has confirmed that its 2027 price rises will be smaller than the 5 to 6% of 2026.

The line runs between scarcity and volume. The most exclusive products hold up; the categories sold in volume suffer. LVMH's fashion and leather goods division fell 5% in the third quarter. Hermès now shows the same split within one house: leather goods grew 11%, while ready-to-wear fell 2% and watches 6%.

"It's the volume divisions that are suffering, so fashion accessories, for example, and also watches and shoes."Hermès CFO, third-quarter revenue call, 22 October 2026

Two engines, two behaviours. Hermès' leather goods work on a supply-constrained model, with demand still above production, and have proved acyclical again. The rest of the house behaves like the luxury sector and moves with the cycle.

What this means for investors. We still see no clear short-term catalyst for the sector without a recovery in China. For Hermès, the quarter showed that the leather core is intact, while the rest of the house slowed with the sector. The debate has moved to the price the market pays for the cyclical half.
