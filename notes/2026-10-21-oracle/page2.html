1. What the company does

Oracle is best known for its database, which runs the core systems of many of the world's largest companies, and for its business applications: Fusion finance, HR and supply-chain software, NetSuite for mid-sized firms, and healthcare software from the former Cerner. Over the past few years it has added a third leg, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI), which rents out computing capacity, much of it for training and running AI models.

The mix is changing fast. In the quarter to 31 August 2026, total revenue rose 30% to USD 19.3 billion. Cloud infrastructure grew 121% to USD 7.4 billion and cloud applications 10% to USD 4.2 billion, while software licence and support revenue fell 3% to USD 5.5 billion as customers move to the cloud.

2. Why it could do well

Demand is ahead of supply. Oracle booked more than USD 30 billion of new AI cloud contracts in the quarter and delivered 850 MW of additional data-center capacity, almost three times the capacity delivered in the previous quarter. Management reported AI infrastructure utilisation of 97.9%, and GPUs coming up for renewal were renewed or resold at an average premium of 20%, a sign that installed capacity keeps its pricing power.

Profits are growing quickly. GAAP operating income rose 57% to USD 6.7 billion and non-GAAP operating income 31% to USD 8.2 billion, a margin of about 42%. Oracle raised its fiscal 2027 guidance to at least USD 90 billion of revenue and non-GAAP earnings per share of USD 8.10.

3. What sets it apart

It owns the whole stack. Database, applications and infrastructure come from one supplier, which gives Oracle a price advantage when customers move Oracle-heavy workloads out of their own data centers or from rival clouds. Oracle does not need to beat AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud on every workload; it needs to win where Oracle data, business applications and AI computing meet.

Multicloud is its most durable asset. Oracle runs its database inside the data centers of AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, so customers can keep their preferred cloud and still buy Oracle. Multicloud database revenue grew 353% year on year and multicloud customers 180%, across 70 regions. Oracle AI Database@AWS alone reached 22 AWS regions in August. The rival clouds thus become distribution channels as well as competitors. The limit is that Oracle may capture the database revenue without the wider infrastructure economics.

A sticky applications base. Cloud applications generated USD 15.9 billion in fiscal 2026, up 11%, and switching costs for finance and HR systems are high. Oracle is now adding governed AI agents to these applications, such as the Fusion Claw runtime launched in September. Their commercial impact is still to be demonstrated.
