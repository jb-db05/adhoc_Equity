1. What the company does

Mitsui is a trading and investment group active in metals and mining, energy, machinery and infrastructure, chemicals, steel products, lifestyle (food, retail, healthcare) and innovation. In the year to March 2026 (FY3/26), profit attributable to shareholders was JPY 834.0 billion. Mineral & Metal Resources earned JPY 253.6 billion, including JPY 43.5 billion of Vale dividends, and Energy JPY 164.2 billion.

2. Why it could do well

First-quarter profit for FY3/27 reached a record JPY 294.1 billion, up from JPY 191.6 billion, and covered 32% of the unchanged JPY 920 billion full-year plan. The plan to FY3/29 targets profit of JPY 1.1 trillion, core operating cash flow of JPY 1.2 trillion and an ROE of 12%. New LNG capacity should help: Mozambique LNG, where Mitsui holds 20%, fully restarted in January 2026, with first LNG expected in 2029.

3. What sets it apart

A clear framework for cash returns, backed by Berkshire Hathaway as a long-term anchor shareholder:

  • around 50% of three-year cumulative core operating cash flow returned to shareholders
  • a dividend floor of JPY 140 a year from FY3/27 to FY3/29, up from JPY 115 in FY3/26
  • a minimum total payout ratio raised to 40% from 37%
  • a buyback of up to JPY 200 billion from August 2026 to January 2027, of which JPY 11.25 billion was done in August

4. Is the share cheap?

Less so than a few years ago. At JPY 5,042, the share trades at about 15.5 times the FY3/27 company plan, 17.3 times FY3/26 earnings per share of JPY 291.12 and about 1.6 times book value. The dividend and buyback add up to a cash return of about 4.2% (Syz calculations). The share sits well below its spring 2026 high but well above its level of a year ago (Chart 1). The valuation now rests more on delivery of the 2029 plan than on a discount to assets.

5. Main risks

  • Commodities. Each USD 1 per tonne on iron ore moves annual profit by about JPY 3 billion. Iron ore and oil prices could soften in 2027 and 2028 as new supply arrives.
  • Yen. Each JPY 1 move against the dollar changes profit by about JPY 4.6 billion. A stronger yen would reduce profit.
  • One-off gains. Part of the first-quarter strength came from gains on investments, which do not recur every quarter.
  • Projects and geopolitics. Mozambique LNG carries security and execution risk. Mitsui's Russian LNG interests (Sakhalin-2, Arctic LNG 2) remain exposed to sanctions.

6. What to watch next

The half-year results on 4 November 2026, and whether the JPY 920 billion plan is revised. After that, the pace of the buyback, iron ore and LNG prices, the yen, and any change in Berkshire's stake. On the chart, a close above JPY 5,333 could confirm an improving trend, while JPY 4,672 is the level to watch on the downside.

CONCLUSION

Mitsui gives an investor a broad portfolio with iron ore and energy at its core, a well-defined return policy with a dividend floor, and an anchor shareholder. Against this, the shares have already re-rated, the first quarter was helped by one-off gains, and earnings depend heavily on commodities whose prices could soften and on the yen. The main question is how much confidence one places in the 2029 plan and how much commodity-cycle volatility one is ready to accept.
