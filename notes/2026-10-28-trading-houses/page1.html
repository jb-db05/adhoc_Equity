Japanese trading houses enter a new phase

From traders to investment groups

Japan's five large trading houses (Mitsubishi Corporation, Mitsui & Co., Itochu, Sumitomo Corporation and Marubeni) have spent the past decade turning from commodity traders into diversified investment groups. Commodities still carry a large weight in profits. A growing share now comes from businesses such as food, power, infrastructure, finance and consumer goods, which earn steadier returns through the cycle.

Capital discipline as the common thread

Each of the large houses now runs a plan that ties dividends and share buybacks to a return on equity goal. The Tokyo Stock Exchange's push for capital efficiency and the arrival of Berkshire Hathaway as a shareholder in all five houses have reinforced this.

A mixed commodity backdrop

Crude oil and iron ore prices could soften in 2027 and 2028 as new supply arrives. Copper stands apart: mine supply remains tight while demand from power grids, electrification and data centres keeps rising. The commodity mix of each house therefore matters more than usual.

Two different mixes

Mitsui is one of the most resource-weighted houses. About half of its profit in the year to March 2026 came from metals and energy (Syz calculation), with iron ore as the largest single driver and LNG next. Marubeni's resource profits lean more on copper in Chile, alongside coking coal and LNG, and it is building scalable platform businesses in agriculture, aircraft leasing, finance and power.

Both houses started the year strongly. Their first-quarter profits each covered 32% of an unchanged full-year plan, and both added large buybacks in August. Both report half-year results on 4 November 2026.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

Both shares pair rising cash returns with earnings that still depend on commodities and the yen. The difference lies in which commodities: iron ore and energy for Mitsui, copper for Marubeni. Holding both adds exposure to the same sector drivers rather than diversification. The half-year results on 4 November are the next checkpoint for both.
