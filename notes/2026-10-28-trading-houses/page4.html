1. What the company does

Marubeni invests in and runs businesses across food and agriculture, energy and power, metals, chemicals, finance and leasing, and infrastructure. Its strategy centres on scalable Strategic Platform Businesses. Examples include Helena, one of the largest agricultural retailers in the United States, Aircastle in aircraft leasing, used-car finance in the United States and SmartestEnergy, a power supplier to large businesses in the United Kingdom. On the resource side, copper in Chile, coking coal in Australia and LNG drive most of the profit.

2. Why it could do well

Net profit reached a record JPY 543.9 billion in the year to March 2026. First-quarter profit for the current year reached JPY 186.4 billion, up 21% and 12% above consensus, helped by copper and coking coal prices and strong chemicals and gas trading. The quarter covers 32% of the unchanged full-year plan of JPY 580 billion. Copper adds volume growth. The second concentrator at Centinela in Chile, where Marubeni holds 30%, could add around 40,000 tonnes a year of attributable copper at full capacity, with first production expected in 2027.

3. What sets it apart

Cash returns are rising quickly. In August, management added a JPY 100 billion buyback to the JPY 45 billion approved in May. It also raised the shareholder return target of its GC2027 plan from JPY 700 billion to JPY 900 billion, and lifted the investment budget to JPY 1.95 trillion. The plan aims for a 15% return on equity. Net debt to equity stood at 0.46x at the end of June.

4. Is the share cheap?

Not against its own history. The share trades at 12.9 times consensus earnings, well above its 10-year average of 7.6 times, and at 14.1 times the company plan (Syz calculation). Much of the re-rating took place between 2023 and early 2026. Since February, the consensus multiple has come back from 18.5 times. This year's share price gain of 16% now matches the 17% rise in consensus earnings. The dividend and this year's buybacks add up to a cash return of around 4%. Further share price moves would depend on earnings growth and buybacks.

5. Main risks

  • Commodities. A sharp fall in copper, coking coal, iron ore or oil prices would weigh on resource profits, which drove much of the recent upside.
  • Yen. A rapid rise of the yen would reduce the value of overseas earnings.
  • Return target. On current guidance, return on equity would reach about 13% this year, so the 15% goal needs higher profits or more buybacks.
  • Investment discipline. The larger investment budget is partly funded by leverage, and a large acquisition at a high price would dilute returns.

6. What to watch next

The half-year results on 4 November 2026, any change to the JPY 580 billion plan and progress on the JPY 100 billion buyback. After that, copper prices, the start-up of the Centinela expansion in 2027, further steps toward the 15% return on equity goal, and new platform investments such as the planned stake in South Korean beauty company iFamilySC announced on 23 October.

CONCLUSION

Marubeni combines record earnings, growing copper volumes and one of the larger capital return programmes among the trading houses. Against this, the share trades above its 10-year valuation range, part of the recent profit rests on commodity prices, and the 15% return goal still needs higher profits or more buybacks. The main question is how far earnings growth and buybacks can keep supporting a valuation that is no longer low against history.
