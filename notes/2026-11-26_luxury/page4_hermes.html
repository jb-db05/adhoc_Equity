The share price is below both its 40-day (EUR 1,303) and 200-day (EUR 1,578) averages, after a 45% fall from the January high to the low of 20 November. A close above EUR 1,357, the 21 October high, could be a first sign of stabilisation; EUR 1,225.5, the low of the year, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

Hermès designs and makes luxury goods in its own workshops, mostly in France: Leather Goods and Saddlery (Birkin, Kelly), nearly half of revenue, then Ready-to-wear and Accessories, Silk and Textiles, Perfumes, Watches, Jewellery and Home. It sells through its own stores, which keeps control of prices and of the customer relationship.

2. Why it could do well

Leather Goods grew 11% in the third quarter in a weak market, thanks to a scarcity model: waiting lists for the Birkin and the Kelly keep demand above supply, and new workshops add capacity each year. The rest of the group is flat, as elsewhere in luxury, with ready-to-wear down 2% and watches down 6%. As the finance director put it, it is the “volume” divisions that are suffering.

3. What sets it apart

A vertically integrated model: the company makes its own products, limits supply and sells to a loyal, wealthy clientele. Its profitability is the highest in the sector, and it has historically held up better than peers in downturns.

4. Is the share cheap?

Cheaper than at any time since 2016, but still expensive against the sector. The shares have lost about 40% this year and trade on about 27 times expected earnings, against a ten-year average of 44.6 times. Growth is slowing: +4.3% in the third quarter against 5% expected, flat in Asia excluding Japan and down to +9% in the Americas. Price increases will be smaller in 2027 than the 5% to 6% of 2026, and analysts have cut their earnings forecasts by 1% to 10%.

5. Main risks

China first: Chinese customers account for more than 40% of sales, the highest exposure in the sector. Some iconic bags are also losing a little of their appeal, with copies and influencers. At about EUR 17bn of sales the group may be too large to avoid the cycle, and a weaker yen reduces reported revenue.

6. What to watch next

Full-year 2026 results in February 2027; the pace of Leather Goods and any recovery among Chinese customers; the pricing decision for 2027; the first men's collection by Grace Wales Bonner and the first Couture collection by Nadège Vanhée in January 2027.

CONCLUSION

Hermès remains the best house in leather goods, where its scarcity model delivered 11% growth in the third quarter, while the rest of the group is flat like the rest of luxury. After a 40% fall the shares trade at their lowest multiple since 2016, yet remain expensive against the sector. The long-term value of the franchise can justify keeping a position, but there is no obvious catalyst in the short term, and China, at more than 40% of sales, is the main risk.
