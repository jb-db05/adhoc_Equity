Exclusivity holds, volume suffers

A third year of slowdown

The luxury sector is in its third consecutive year of slowdown, and the gap between brands keeps widening. Jewellery and the most exclusive houses are holding up, while fashion and leather goods are suffering. The third quarter showed the split clearly: Hermès grew its Leather Goods by 11% on the back of waiting lists for the Birkin and the Kelly, while LVMH's Fashion & Leather Goods, home to Louis Vuitton and Dior, fell 5%. At LVMH, jewellery, Sephora and spirits grew 6% to 8%, while at Hermès the categories sold in volume, such as ready-to-wear and watches, declined.

Years of strong price increases explain part of the split. Aspirational buyers have moved to cheaper brands or to second-hand, and the customers who remain favour the rarest products.

China no longer drives growth

The Chinese luxury market fell 3% to 5% in 2025, after a decline of 17% to 19% in 2024. In the third quarter, Asia excluding Japan declined at LVMH and was flat at Hermès. The American customer is now the last engine of the sector: LVMH grew 5% in the United States and Hermès 9% in the Americas, although the pace is slowing.

No catalyst in the short term

Nothing points to a quick improvement. The fourth quarter could be difficult, with France suffering (Hermès grew 4% there in the third quarter, against 10% in the rest of Europe), the Middle East not recovering and no visible improvement in China. A strong euro also reduces the reported revenue of European groups.

Valuations have reset

Share prices have adjusted faster than earnings. LVMH has lost 38% since the start of the year and trades on about 17 times expected earnings, against 20 to 25 times historically. Hermès has lost about 40% and trades on about 27 times, its lowest level since 2016. Both multiples are near ten-year lows, but analysts are still cutting their 2027 forecasts, so a low multiple alone does not mark the bottom.

What this means for investors

In a split market, the strength of the franchise matters more than the cycle. Hermès remains on the right side of the split through its leather goods, while the core of LVMH is on the wrong side for now. Valuations already discount a lot, but with no short-term catalyst and estimates still falling, the full-year results of LVMH in late January 2027 and of Hermès in February 2027 are the next data points.
