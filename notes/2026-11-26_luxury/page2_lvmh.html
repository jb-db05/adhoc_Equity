The shares are back above their 40-day average (EUR 392.6) but far below a still-falling 200-day average (EUR 455.6). A close above EUR 416, the early-November high, could signal a stabilisation; EUR 380, near the October low, is the level to watch on the downside.

1. What the company does

LVMH is the world's largest luxury group, with about 75 houses in five divisions: Fashion & Leather Goods (Louis Vuitton, Dior, Loro Piana, Celine), Perfumes & Cosmetics, Watches & Jewelry (Tiffany, Bulgari), Selective Retailing (Sephora) and Wines & Spirits (Moët & Chandon, Hennessy). Revenue was EUR 80.8bn in 2025.

2. Why it could do well

The other divisions are growing: in the third quarter Selective Retailing rose 8% and Wines & Spirits 6%, and Watches & Jewelry grew 8% over nine months. The group also has levers of its own: creative renewal at Dior, newness at Louis Vuitton, cost discipline and a leaner portfolio, with Marc Jacobs sold in September and the stake in SirDavis whisky in July. Operating free cash flow reached EUR 11.3bn in 2025.

3. What sets it apart

Few groups own as many brands with pricing power, control their own stores and production, and can keep investing through a downturn. Net debt of EUR 8.2bn at the end of June was less than half of 2025 operating profit, and control by the Arnault family gives the group a long-term horizon.

4. Cheap, or a value trap?

Valuation is the only reason for patience. At about 17 times expected earnings, against 20 to 25 times historically, the multiple is at its lowest in about ten years. Earnings are now the risk. Fashion & Leather Goods, about 70% of group operating profit, fell 5% in the third quarter against about −2% expected, and management rules out price cuts and sees no shortcut back to growth. As long as organic growth does not recover, the margin will be hard to defend, and analysts keep lowering their 2027 forecasts.

5. Main risks

On the upside, a quick rebound if Louis Vuitton stabilises sooner than expected, as the shares are heavily oversold. On the downside, if the margin gives way, estimates would fall and the shares would no longer look so cheap. Also a longer slump in China, where the Molly Tea dispute hurt the brand; aspirational customers trading down; a strong euro and tariffs; succession (Bernard Arnault is 77).

6. What to watch next

Full-year results in late January 2027, especially the Fashion & Leather Goods margin; Chinese demand; the new Dior collections and newness at Louis Vuitton; revisions to 2027 estimates.

CONCLUSION

The core of LVMH is shrinking: Fashion & Leather Goods fell 5% in the third quarter and group growth slowed to 1%, and Sephora, jewellery and spirits cannot make up for it. Valuation is the only reason to wait, with the multiple near a ten-year low and the shares down nearly 40% this year. The selling looks like capitulation, which is never a comfortable moment to sell, but a choice will have to be made at some point: without a return to organic growth, the margin and 2027 forecasts remain under pressure.
