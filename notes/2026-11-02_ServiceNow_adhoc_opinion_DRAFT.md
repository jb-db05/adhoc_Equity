## Software and the AI debate

**Left column**

Software stocks have been through a long and deep correction. Since early 2025, investors have increasingly priced in the risk that AI agents will take over tasks that companies currently pay software vendors for, licence by licence. This would erode the subscription model on which the sector has been built. ServiceNow, long seen as one of the highest-quality names in the sector, has not been spared: its share has lost more than half of its value from its January 2025 peak.

The reaction to third-quarter results on 29 October shows how fragile sentiment remains. ServiceNow and Fortinet both fell sharply after their releases, and the move spread to other large software names such as Salesforce and Workday.

**Right column**

The market is no longer willing to pay in advance for growth. Software vendors that sit deep inside their customers' processes, and that can sell AI on top of an existing platform, could still turn the shift to their advantage. Until AI visibly lifts overall growth, however, investors are likely to treat every slowdown as a warning rather than a pause.

**What this means for investors**

Lower valuations alone are not enough to call the bottom in software. The sector has moved from a story investors paid up for to one they need to see proven. The quality of the customer base, cash generation and balance-sheet strength are now the main differences between names, and patience is likely to be required.

## ServiceNow (NOW US)

**Subtitle (orange):** Down more than half from its peak, the share now has to earn back the market's confidence

**KPI tiles**

| Price | From all-time high | 2026 subscription revenue growth (guidance) | 2026 free cash flow margin (guidance) |
|---|---|---|---|
| USD 108.23* | about -54%* | 21% (constant currency) | 36% |

**Left column**

**1. What the company does**
ServiceNow sells a cloud platform that large companies use to run and automate their internal workflows, starting with IT and extending to HR, customer service, security and finance. It now positions itself as the layer that coordinates and controls AI agents across a company, above the applications they already use.

**2. Why it could do well**
AI is starting to contribute. Management has raised its target for annual contract value from AI products twice this year, to more than USD 1.7bn. Because ServiceNow already holds the data and workflows of its customers, it can sell AI to an installed base rather than win new clients from scratch. The company also generates a great deal of cash: free cash flow was USD 4.5bn in 2025, and management now targets a 36% margin for 2026, up from 35%.

**3. What sets it apart**
The customer base is large and loyal. ServiceNow works with a large majority of the Fortune 500 and has historically renewed around 98% of its contracts. Customers that use several of its products would find it costly and disruptive to replace them. This protects existing revenue, but it does not by itself guarantee the pace of future growth, which is what the market is now questioning.

**Right column**

**4. Cheap, or a value trap?**
The share peaked at about USD 234 in January 2025 (adjusted for the five-for-one split of December 2025). It fell to about USD 83 in April 2026, recovered to around USD 150 ahead of the results, and has given up most of that rebound since. At around USD 110bn of market value, it trades at roughly 18 to 19 times the free cash flow expected for 2026 (Syz calculation), well below its historical range. This is not a sign of a broken business: revenue still grows at about 20% a year and cash generation keeps improving. But a derating of this scale reflects a lasting change in how the market values software. A lower multiple could prove justified if growth keeps slowing, so the valuation alone is not enough to make the case.

**5. Main risks**
- Contracted revenue for the next twelve months (cRPO) is expected to grow 18.5% in constant currency in Q4, after 21% in Q3. Management points to technical factors (more on-premise contracts, early renewals pulled into Q3), but a further slowdown in 2027 would weaken that explanation.
- Acquisitions, led by Armis (cybersecurity, USD 7.75bn), have been financed partly with debt. They weigh on margins in the short term and make the underlying growth of the core business harder to read.
- AI brings additional computing costs, which could limit margin gains.
- Simpler and cheaper competitors are active among mid-sized companies.
- Sentiment towards software could stay negative regardless of company results, as the past two years have shown.

**6. What to watch next**
- Q4 2026 results (late January 2027): actual cRPO growth against the 18.5% guidance, and the first targets for 2027.
- Signs that AI is lifting overall subscription growth, not only its own line.
- Progress on integrating Armis and reducing the acquisition debt.

## Conclusion

The third quarter was not a weak quarter in itself: subscription revenue grew 21% to USD 3.99bn, and management raised its full-year targets for revenue, cash flow and AI. But the reaction confirmed that the market no longer gives ServiceNow the benefit of the doubt. A share that has lost more than half of its value in less than two years is signalling doubts about the long-term growth of the model, not about one quarter. ServiceNow keeps real strengths: an embedded customer base, strong cash generation and a credible AI offer. These are what separate it from a company in difficulty. Restoring confidence, however, is likely to take time and several quarters of stable or improving growth, and the share could remain volatile in the meantime.
