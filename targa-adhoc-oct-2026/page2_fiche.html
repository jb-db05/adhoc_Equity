1. What the company does

Targa is the largest gatherer and processor of natural gas in the Permian Basin. Its gathering and processing business collects gas at the wellhead and separates out the liquids. Its logistics business then moves the NGLs by pipeline to its fractionation hub at Mont Belvieu, Texas, where they are split into products, and exports LPG from its Galena Park terminal near Houston. Most earnings come from fees, but part of the processing business is still paid as a share of the products sold, which keeps some exposure to commodity prices.

2. Why it could do well

Volumes and contracts. Second-quarter adjusted EBITDA reached a record USD 1.60bn, up 38% on a year earlier, and management expects 2026 to land at the top end of its USD 5.7 to 5.9bn range. In August, Targa signed 20-year agreements with ExxonMobil covering Delaware and Midland acreage, with three new processing plants (about 825 MMcf/d) and a new gas pipeline due in the first half of 2028, and up to five more plants under study. The 25% dividend increase signals confidence in future cash flow.

3. What sets it apart

Integration. Keeping molecules on its own system from the well to the dock lets Targa earn a fee at each step and capture marketing gains when prices differ between regions. Acreage dedications from leading Permian producers, now including a 20-year anchor with ExxonMobil, give volume visibility into the 2040s. Leverage of 3.4x is comfortable for the sector.

4. Is the share cheap?

No. At 26.8x forward earnings, Targa trades above its median of 21.6x since 2021. Unlike some peers, the rise rests on real earnings growth: consensus forward EPS has nearly doubled since January 2023, from about USD 5.97 to USD 11.35. A re-rating from about 12x to 27x did the rest, and the share has quadrupled. Part of the recent strength is less repeatable: press reports put second-quarter marketing gains, linked to regional price gaps, at around USD 250m, and these could fade as new pipelines ease bottlenecks. The 1.6% dividend yield offers little support if sentiment turns.

5. Main risks

Valuation first: after a 65% rise this year, a slowdown in Permian drilling or a fall in oil prices could hit both earnings estimates and the multiple. Targa still has more commodity exposure than most large US midstream groups. Growth capital of about USD 5bn in 2026 keeps free cash flow thin until large projects start up. Long lead times for turbines and compressors could delay new plants.

6. What to watch next

Third-quarter results on 5 November, and whether 2026 EBITDA comes in above the range. The 2027 outlook, expected early in 2027, for capital spending and the timing of higher free cash flow. Progress on the ExxonMobil plants and any decision on the additional plants under study.

CONCLUSION

We like Targa as the best-positioned operator in the Permian, with earnings growth that justifies part of its re-rating. The share, however, sits at an all-time high and a premium multiple, which leaves little room for disappointment. An investor who holds it could take partial profits to bring the weight back to target, keeping a core position for its growth. An investor without exposure could build a position gradually, after the third-quarter results, rather than chase the recent rise. Holders of Energy Transfer or Williams already have overlapping US midstream exposure.
