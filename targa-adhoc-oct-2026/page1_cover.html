Permian midstream: more gas, more liquids, more pipes

High oil prices keep Permian drilling busy

The Permian Basin in west Texas and New Mexico is the largest US oil field. Each barrel of oil there comes with natural gas and natural gas liquids (NGLs) such as ethane and propane, which must be gathered, processed and moved to the Gulf Coast. With Brent above USD 100 a barrel for most of 2026, producers have kept drilling and these volumes keep rising.

Pipeline capacity has struggled to keep up. Bottlenecks around the Waha hub in west Texas have at times pushed local gas prices below zero in recent years, and new pipelines are being built to carry the gas toward Texas and Louisiana markets.

Exports pull the chain

US exports of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) keep growing. Companies that own the full chain, from the gas plant to the export dock, can collect a fee at every step and profit when regional prices diverge. Targa is one of a handful of operators that own the whole chain.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

The Permian offers the fastest volume growth in US midstream, but it remains tied to oil drilling. The question for an investor is how much of that growth is already priced: the best-positioned names now trade at premium multiples.
