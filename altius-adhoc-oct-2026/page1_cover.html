Royalty companies: revenue without running the mine

How royalties work

A royalty company provides upfront money to a mine or energy project and in return receives a share of its revenue for many years, without paying operating or expansion costs. Margins are high and the model scales easily, but the royalty holder has no say in how or when the asset is developed.

Most listed royalty companies focus on gold and silver. A smaller group, including Altius, focuses on base metals, fertilisers, battery metals and, increasingly, renewable power.

Renewables and lithium: two different cycles

Renewable power royalties pay out as wind and solar farms produce electricity, which makes them steady once projects are built. Lithium royalties behave very differently: lithium prices collapsed in 2023 and 2024 and remain volatile, so revenue swings with the market.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INVESTORS

Royalty companies are usually valued on the worth of their royalties rather than on current earnings, and tend to trade at a premium for their low costs and diversification. The question for an investor is whether the growth now coming from renewables and lithium justifies that premium.
