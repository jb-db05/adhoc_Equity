1. What the company does

Altius owns royalties on mines and power projects run by others. Its portfolio includes base metal royalties, notably on the Chapada copper mine in Brazil; potash royalties in Saskatchewan; iron ore interests in Labrador; lithium royalties acquired with Lithium Royalty Corp in March 2026; and a 50% stake in Great Bay Renewables, which funds US wind, solar and storage projects in exchange for revenue royalties. A project generation unit stakes mineral ground, sells it to developers and keeps royalties.

2. Why it could do well

Growth is coming from new assets. Preliminary third-quarter attributable revenue reached a record CAD 33.2m, up 55% on a year earlier, with electricity now the largest contributor. In 2026, Altius bought Lithium Royalty Corp for about CAD 520m, raised its Great Bay Renewables stake to 50% and enlarged its credit facility, while a CAD 181.5m share issue in July strengthened the balance sheet. The Kami iron ore project, the Curipamba mine in Ecuador and the Arthur gold project in Nevada could add value over the next few years.

3. What sets it apart

Origination. Altius creates many of its royalties itself at low cost through project generation. The clearest example is Arthur, where it sold a 1% royalty to Franco-Nevada in 2025 for up to USD 275m while keeping 0.5%. Its focus on base metals, fertilisers and renewables, rather than gold, also sets it apart from most royalty peers.

4. Cheap, or a value trap?

Neither clearly. At 66.7x forward earnings, the share is still above its median of 57.3x since 2021, even after falling 26% since 22 September. Forward EPS has roughly doubled since the end of 2025, from CAD 0.39 to CAD 0.76, on the back of acquisitions. The record quarter needs context: about CAD 9m of the CAD 33.2m came from interest and investment income, mainly on financing for renewable projects, rather than from royalties, and lithium revenue fell from the second quarter. Royalty companies are usually valued on their assets rather than on earnings, but the correction has removed only part of the premium. The dividend yield is below 1%.

5. Main risks

Altius has no control over the projects it depends on: timelines for Kami, Curipamba or Arthur rest with their operators. Revenue remains sensitive to copper, potash and especially lithium prices. The renewables business depends on US power markets and policy toward wind and solar. Acquisitions were paid partly in shares, and the July share issue at CAD 60.50 is now above the market price, which can weigh on sentiment. No clear cause has been given for the recent fall, which makes the short term hard to read.

6. What to watch next

Full third-quarter results on 10 November, including the contribution of the consolidated renewables business. Feasibility work on Kami and Arthur. First production at Curipamba, expected in 2027. Any new acquisition, and how it is financed.

CONCLUSION

We see Altius as a well-run royalty company whose pivot to renewables and lithium adds growth, but also new sources of volatility. The recent fall offers a better entry point than in September, yet the valuation still assumes the new assets deliver, and the record quarter was flattered by interest income. An investor without exposure could build a position gradually, using weakness rather than buying in one go. An investor who holds it could keep the position and wait for the full third-quarter results before adding.
